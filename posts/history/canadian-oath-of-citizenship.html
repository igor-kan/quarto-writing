The Canadian Oath of Citizenship: Linguistic Analysis, French Phonetics (IPA), and Constitutional Symbolism

A comprehensive line-by-line guide with IPA transcription, literal word-for-word translation, and historical-constitutional analysis of the Crown and Indigenous treaties.

Civics
Law
Linguistics
Canadian History
French
Published

September 8, 2026

The Oath of Citizenship (Serment de citoyenneté) is the legally binding declaration through which an immigrant formally enters the Canadian political community. Administered by a Citizenship Judge or presiding official under the Citizenship Act, reciting the oath is the mandatory final step to obtaining Canadian citizenship.

On June 21, 2021, the Parliament of Canada enacted Bill C-8, updating the oath to fulfill Call to Action 94 of the Truth and Reconciliation Commission by explicitly recognizing the constitutional and treaty rights of First Nations, Inuit, and Métis peoples. Following the accession of King Charles III on September 8, 2022, the royal title in the oath transitioned to His Majesty King Charles the Third, King of Canada.

This article provides an exhaustive linguistic, phonetic, and constitutional breakdown of the Oath in both official languages.

1. Official Texts: English and French

  • English Version
  • Version Française

“I swear (or affirm)
that I will be faithful
and bear true allegiance
to His Majesty King Charles the Third, King of Canada,
His Heirs and Successors,
and that I will faithfully observe the laws of Canada,
including the Constitution,
which recognizes and affirms the Aboriginal and treaty rights of First Nations, Inuit and Métis peoples,
and fulfil my duties as a Canadian citizen.”

« Je jure (ou j’affirme solennellement)
que je serai fidèle
et porterai sincère allégeance
à Sa Majesté le roi Charles Trois, roi du Canada,
à ses héritiers et successeurs,
que j’observerai fidèlement les lois du Canada,
y compris la Constitution,
qui reconnaît et confirme les droits ancestraux ou issus de traités des Premières Nations, des Inuits et des Métis,
et que je remplirai loyalement mes obligations de citoyen canadien [citoyenne canadienne]. »

2. Line-by-Line French Phonetic Guide (IPA) & Literal Word-for-Word Translation

Below is a stanza-by-stanza breakdown of the French oath, providing the International Phonetic Alphabet (IPA) transcription (Standard Canadian/International French), an interlinear word-for-word literal translation, and a natural English translation.

Stanza 1: The Declaration / L’Engagement

French:   Je    jure   (ou   j'affirme              solennellement)
IPA:      /ʒə   ʒyʁ/   (/u   ʒ‿a.fiʁm               sɔ.lan.ɛl.mɑ̃/)
Literal:  I     swear  (or   I-affirm               solemnly)
Natural:  I swear (or solemnly affirm)
  • Phonetic Notes:
    • /ʒyʁ/ (jure): The French /y/ is a high front rounded vowel (shape lips for oo as in “boot”, but pronounce ee as in “beet”).
    • sɔ.lan.ɛl.mɑ̃ (solennellement): Note the irregular pronunciation where the letter e before double n is pronounced /a/ (similar to femme /fam/).
    • Final /ɑ̃/: Low back nasal vowel.

Stanza 2: The Fealty / La Fidélité

French:   que   je    serai   fidèle
IPA:      /kə   ʒə    sə.ʁe   fi.dɛl/
Literal:  that  I     will-be faithful
Natural:  that I will be faithful
  • Phonetic Notes:
    • sə.ʁe (serai): Simple future first-person singular of être. The final -ai is pronounced as a close-mid /e/ (or open-mid /ɛ/ depending on regional dialect).
    • fi.dɛl (fidèle): The final consonant /l/ is clearly articulated; the silent e is dropped.

Stanza 3: Allegiance to the Crown / L’Allégeance à la Couronne

French:   et   porterai    sincère   allégeance
IPA:      /e   pɔʁ.tə.ʁe   sɛ̃.sɛʁ    a.le.ʒɑ̃s/
Literal:  and  will-bear   sincere   allegiance
Natural:  and bear true allegiance
  • Phonetic Notes:
    • pɔʁ.tə.ʁe (porterai): The literal verb is porter (“to carry/bear”).
    • sɛ̃.sɛʁ (sincère): Starts with the front open-mid nasal vowel /ɛ̃/.
    • a.le.ʒɑ̃s (allégeance): Ends with the back nasal vowel /ɑ̃/ followed by unvoiced /s/.

Stanza 4: The Sovereign / Le Souverain

French:   à   Sa    Majesté   le   roi   Charles   Trois,   roi   du    Canada,
IPA:      /a  sa    ma.ʒɛs.te  lə   ʁwa   ʃaʁl      tʁwa     ʁwa   dy    ka.na.da/
Literal:  to  Her/His Majesty  the  king  Charles   Three,   king  of-the Canada,
Natural:  to His Majesty King Charles the Third, King of Canada,
  • Phonetic Notes:
    • ʁwa (roi): Starts with the French voiced uvular fricative /ʁ/ followed by the semi-vowel glide /w/ and /a/.
    • ʃaʁl (Charles): Single syllable; the terminal e is silent in standard speech.
    • tʁwa (Trois): In French royal nomenclature, cardinal numbers (Trois = Three) are used rather than ordinal numbers (Troisième = Third), except for Premier (the First).
    • dy (du): Contraction of de + le. High front rounded vowel /y/.

Stanza 5: Heirs & Successors / Les Héritiers et Successeurs

French:   à   ses    héritiers      et   successeurs,
IPA:      /a  se     z‿e.ʁi.tje     e    syk.sɛ.sœʁ/
Literal:  to  his    heirs          and  successors,
Natural:  to His Heirs and Successors,
  • Phonetic & Liaison Alert:
    • Obligatory Liaison: ses héritiers is pronounced /se z‿e.ʁi.tje/. The silent s of ses links to the initial vowel of héritiers (silent h) as a voiced /z/ sound.
    • syk.sɛ.sœʁ (successeurs): Double c produces /ks/. Ends with the open-mid front rounded vowel /œ/.

Stanza 6: Obeying the Laws / Le Respect des Lois

French:   que   j'observerai       fidèlement   les   lois   du    Canada,
IPA:      /kə   ʒ‿ɔb.zɛʁ.və.ʁe     fi.dɛl.mɑ̃    le    lwa    dy    ka.na.da/
Literal:  that  I-will-observe     faithfully   the   laws   of-the Canada,
Natural:  that I will faithfully observe the laws of Canada,
  • Phonetic Notes:
    • ʒ‿ɔb.zɛʁ.və.ʁe (j’observerai): The letter b before s often assimilates partially to unvoiced /p/ or voiced /b/ in Canadian French (/ɔb.zɛʁ.və.ʁe/).
    • fi.dɛl.mɑ̃ (fidèlement): Nasal ending /mɑ̃/.

Stanza 7: The Constitution / La Constitution

French:   y       compris      la   Constitution,
IPA:      /i      kɔ̃.pʁi       la   kɔ̃s.ti.ty.sjɔ̃/
Literal:  there   included     the  Constitution,
Natural:  including the Constitution,
  • Phonetic Notes:
    • i kɔ̃.pʁi (y compris): Standard idiom for “including”. Nasal vowel /ɔ̃/.
    • kɔ̃s.ti.ty.sjɔ̃ (Constitution): Features two occurrences of the back nasal vowel /ɔ̃/ bookending /ty/ (/y/). The suffix -tion is pronounced /sjɔ̃/.

Stanza 8: Indigenous & Treaty Rights (2021 Bill C-8 Amendment)

French:   qui   reconnaît      et    confirme   les    droits   ancestraux
IPA:      /ki   ʁə.kɔ.nɛ       e     kɔ̃.fiʁm    le     dʁwa     z‿ɑ̃.sɛs.tʁo/
Literal:  which recognizes     and   confirms   the    rights   ancestral

French:   ou   issus          de   traités     des    Premières   Nations,
IPA:      /u   i.sy           də   tʁɛ.te      de     pʁə.mjɛʁ    na.sjɔ̃/
Literal:  or   derived/issued of   treaties    of-the First       Nations,

French:   des    Inuits    et   des    Métis,
IPA:      /de    i.nɥit    e    de     me.tis/
Literal:  of-the Inuits    and  of-the Métis,
  • Phonetic & Pronunciation Keys:
    • Liaison: droits ancestraux \(\to\) /dʁwa z‿ɑ̃.sɛs.tʁo/ (the plural s of droits links as a /z/).
    • ɑ̃.sɛs.tʁo (ancestraux): Irregular plural of ancestral. Ends in close-mid back vowel /o/.
    • i.nɥit (Inuits): In Canadian English and French, the final t of Inuit is articulated: /i.nɥit/. The singular is Inuk, though Inuit serves collectively.
    • me.tis (Métis): In standard Canadian French, the terminal s in Métis is pronounced: /me.tis/ (not silent).

Stanza 9: Duties of Citizenship / Les Obligations Civiques

French:   et   que   je    remplirai      loyalement   mes    obligations
IPA:      /e   kə    ʒə    ʁɑ̃.pli.ʁe      lwa.jal.mɑ̃   me     z‿ɔ.bli.ɡa.sjɔ̃/
Literal:  and  that  I     will-fulfill   loyally      my     obligations

French:   de   citoyen           canadien        [citoyenne          canadienne]
IPA:      /də  si.twa.jɛ̃         ka.na.djɛ̃/      [/si.twa.jɛn        ka.na.djɛn/]
Literal:  of   citizen(masc)     Canadian(masc)  [citizen(fem)       Canadian(fem)]
Natural:  and fulfil my duties as a Canadian citizen.
  • Phonetic & Gender Notes:
    • Liaison: mes obligations \(\to\) /me z‿ɔ.bli.ɡa.sjɔ̃/.
    • Gender Agreement: Candidates choose the masculine or feminine form:
      • Masculine: citoyen canadien /si.twa.jɛ̃ ka.na.djɛ̃/ (nasal endings /jɛ̃/).
      • Feminine: citoyenne canadienne /si.twa.jɛn ka.na.djɛn/ (non-nasal oral vowel /ɛ/ followed by articulated /n/).

3. Constitutional Context and Symbolic Analysis

1. Why Swear Allegiance to the Monarch?

Newcomers to Canada often ask: Why do I pledge allegiance to an individual king residing in London, rather than to the country, the flag, or the constitution?

In Canada’s Westminster constitutional parliamentary democracy, the Sovereign is the personification of the State:

\[\text{The Sovereign} \equiv \text{The Crown in Right of Canada} \equiv \text{The Body Politic / The Canadian People}\]

  • The Crown as a Legal Concept: In Canadian constitutional law, King Charles III reigns not in his personal capacity, but strictly as King of Canada—a legal institution legally distinct from the monarchy of the United Kingdom.
  • Separation of State from Politics: In republics (such as the United States), citizens swear an oath to the Constitution. In Canada, swearing fealty to a non-partisan constitutional monarch ensures that a citizen’s loyalty is pledged to the enduring rule of law and the democratic institution itself, completely detached from whichever political party or Prime Minister currently holds executive power.
  • Judicial Confirmation (Roach v. Canada, 1994): The Ontario Court of Appeal ruled that the oath to the Sovereign is an oath to a system of democratic governance and fundamental freedoms under the Crown, not a declaration of personal subjugation to an individual.

2. “His Heirs and Successors”

The phrase His Heirs and Successors (ses héritiers et successeurs) guarantees the continuity of the state. Under the constitutional maxim “The King never dies” (Le Roi est mort, vive le Roi!), executive authority passes instantly to the successor without any interregnum or vacuum in governance. When Queen Elizabeth II passed away, existing Canadian citizens did not need to re-swear allegiance; the original oath automatically transferred to King Charles III by operation of law.

3. Swear vs. Solemnly Affirm

Under Section 2(a) of the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms (Freedom of Conscience and Religion), citizenship candidates may choose between: 1. The Religious Oath (Serment): Candidates hold a sacred text (the Bible, Quran, Tanakh, Bhagavad Gita, Guru Granth Sahib, etc.) and say “I swear” (Je jure), concluding optionally with “So help me God” (Que Dieu me vienne en aide). 2. The Secular Solemn Affirmation (Affirmation solennelle): Candidates who do not adhere to a religious faith or whose religious convictions forbid swearing oaths (such as Quakers or Mennonites) say “I solemnly affirm” (J’affirme solennellement). Both declarations carry identical legal force.

4. The 2021 Bill C-8 Amendment: Indigenous Treaty Rights

Prior to 2021, the oath contained no mention of Canada’s Indigenous peoples. In 2015, the Truth and Reconciliation Commission of Canada (TRC) issued 94 Calls to Action to redress the legacy of residential schools and advance national reconciliation.

Call to Action 94 specifically recommended amending the Oath of Citizenship to reflect the foundational role of Indigenous peoples: > “We call upon the Government of Canada to replace the Oath of Citizenship with the following: ‘…and that I will faithfully observe the laws of Canada including Treaties with Indigenous Peoples…’”

Parliament codified this through Bill C-8, which received Royal Assent on June 21, 2021 (National Indigenous Peoples Day). The added clause: > “…including the Constitution, which recognizes and affirms the Aboriginal and treaty rights of First Nations, Inuit and Métis peoples…”

  • Constitutional Reference: This clause explicitly references Section 35 of the Constitution Act, 1982, which constitutionalized Aboriginal and treaty rights, entrenching the principle that Canada’s sovereignty exists in a nation-to-nation partnership with Indigenous peoples.
  • Civic Duty for New Canadians: By speaking these words, new citizens formally recognize that they are settling on ancestral lands governed by historic and modern treaties, transforming immigration into an act of shared civic reconciliation.

4. Practical Checklist for the Citizenship Ceremony

  1. Bilingual Requirement: At the official ceremony, candidates are expected to repeat the oath in both English and French after the presiding Citizenship Officer or Judge.
  2. Right Hand Raised: Candidates raise their right hand while reciting the words.
  3. Affirmation of Signature: Following oral recitation, candidates sign the formal paper or digital Oath document, certifying that they have fulfilled the conditions of Section 24 of the Citizenship Act.
  4. Acquisition of Rights: The instant the oath is concluded and signed, the candidate possesses all constitutional rights of Canadian citizens, including the right to enter and remain in Canada (Section 6), the right to vote in federal and provincial elections (Section 3), and the eligibility to hold a Canadian passport.
