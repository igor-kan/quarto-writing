Executive Summary & Strategic Project Framework

As statistical AI models and code-generation copilots commoditize routine programming and cookbook mathematical manipulations, undergraduate STEM students face a critical strategic choice:

How can a student select a semester research or capstone project that builds durable, un-hallucinable expertise that professors respect and top industry employers actively recruit?

To solve this, every project blueprint across ASTB23 (Astrophysics), PHYC54 (Classical Mechanics), PHYD57 (Advanced Computational Methods), and STAC67 (Regression Analysis) is formulated strictly against five fundamental criteria:

  1. Semester Feasibility: Scoped to be independently designed, executed, analyzed, and written up within a standard 12-week academic term by a single student.
  2. Curricular Grounding: Directly anchors and extends the official course syllabus, lecture schedule, and canonical textbooks (Taylor, Kutner, Srinivasan).
  3. AI Automation Resilience: Develops deep foundational skills that statistical AI systems fundamentally lack:
    • Handling coordinate singularities and asymptotic boundary conditions.
    • Preserving geometric and symplectic invariants (Lie groups, phase-space forms).
    • Profiling and optimizing against physical hardware constraints (GPU shared memory, cache lines, SIMD registers).
    • Navigating post-selection inference, observational biases, and non-diagonal covariance kernels.
  4. Academic Leverage (Impressing Faculty): Demonstrates graduate-level theoretical maturity, analytical rigor, and clean scientific code that immediately distinguishes the student for NSERC USRA awards, faculty research positions, and top graduate school recommendations.
  5. Industry & Professional Value: Cultivates production-grade numerical modeling, HPC systems engineering, Bayesian estimation, and econometric inference directly applicable to high-paying roles in quantitative finance, aerospace engineering, defense simulation, scientific machine learning, and biostatistics.

🌌 ASTB23H3: Stars, Galaxies & the Universe

Instructor: Prof. Pawel Artymowicz · Key Topics: Hydrostatic equilibrium, polytropes, Chandrasekhar limit, galactic rotation curves, epicyclic frequencies, dark matter.

Project 1: Relativistic Fermi-Dirac Polytrope Solver at the Chandrasekhar Boundary

  • The Physical Problem: White dwarfs transition from non-relativistic (\(n = 1.5\), \(P \propto \rho^{5/3}\)) to ultra-relativistic degeneracy (\(n = 3\), \(P \propto \rho^{4/3}\)) as mass approaches the Chandrasekhar limit (\(M \to 1.44 M_\odot\)). The Lane-Emden equation contains an essential coordinate singularity at \(\xi = 0\).
  • Implementation & Deliverables:
    1. Derive 6th-order Taylor series expansion around \(\xi = 0\) to initialize numerical shooting off the singular boundary.
    2. Implement an adaptive Runge-Kutta-Fehlberg (RK45) integrator with relativistic Fermi-Dirac equation of state and Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff (TOV) relativistic corrections.
    3. Reconstruct the complete \(M(R)\) mass-radius curve, explicitly identifying the relativistic instability threshold.
  • Why It’s AI-Resilient: LLMs routinely crash or hallucinate false approximations when handling singular boundary values (\(\frac{0}{0}\)) and relativistic thermodynamic integrals.

Project 2: Bayesian MCMC Rotation Curve Decomposition with Real Gaia DR3 / SPARC Data

  • The Physical Problem: The “Cusp-vs-Core Problem”—standard cold dark matter cosmological simulations predict cuspy central density profiles (\(\rho_{\text{NFW}} \propto r^{-1}\)), whereas observations of dwarf galaxies frequently favor cored profiles (\(\rho_{\text{Burkert}} \to \text{const}\)).
  • Implementation & Deliverables:
    1. Ingest observational rotation curves from the SPARC database and local stellar kinematics from Gaia DR3.
    2. Implement analytical velocity profiles for Freeman thin disks, Hernquist bulges, NFW cusps, and Burkert cores.
    3. Execute Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) and Nested Sampling (emcee / dynesty) to estimate joint posteriors for halo parameters and stellar mass-to-light ratios \((\Upsilon_{\text{disk}}, \Upsilon_{\text{bulge}})\).
    4. Compute Bayes factors (\(\ln B_{\text{core, cusp}}\)) across dwarf vs giant galaxies.
  • Career Impact: Quantitative finance, data science, and academic astrophysics all revolve around rigorous Bayesian parameter estimation from noisy observational data.

🌀 PHYC54H3: Advanced Classical Mechanics

Instructor: Prof. Johann Bayer · Key Topics: Calculus of variations, constraints, non-inertial frames, rigid body Euler equations, Hamiltonian phase space.

Project 1: Symplectic Lie-Poisson Integrator for the Dzhanibekov Effect & Asymmetric Top Chaos

  • The Physical Problem: In Taylor Chapter 10, Euler’s equations reveal that rotation about the intermediate principal axis (\(I_1 < I_2 < I_3\)) is violently unstable (the Dzhanibekov Effect / tennis racket theorem). Standard Runge-Kutta integrators suffer from artificial energy drift and violate angular momentum conservation over long integration times, while Euler angles suffer from gimbal lock.
  • Implementation & Deliverables:
    1. Formulate rigid body dynamics on the dual Lie algebra \(\mathfrak{so}(3)^*\), preserving Casimir invariants \(\|\mathbf{L}\|^2\) by construction.
    2. Implement a symplectic quaternion / rotor geometric integrator updating orientation on the smooth manifold \(\mathrm{SO}(3)\) without coordinate poles.
    3. Benchmark numerical phase portraits against exact analytical solutions in terms of Jacobi elliptic functions (\(\text{sn}, \text{cn}, \text{dn}\)).
    4. Compute Lyapunov exponents mapping the chaotic separatrix.
  • Career Impact: Direct application to aerospace satellite attitude determination and control systems (ADCS at NASA, SpaceX, MDA Space) and geometric robotics.

Project 2: Non-Inertial Geostrophic Turbulence & Atmospheric Rossby Wave Solver on a Rotating Sphere

  • The Physical Problem: Scaling Taylor Chapter 9 (Coriolis and centrifugal forces) to continuum planetary atmospheres (Taylor Chapter 16), where latitude-dependent Coriolis acceleration (\(f = 2\Omega\sin\phi\)) generates planetary Rossby waves and jet streams.
  • Implementation & Deliverables:
    1. Formulate the quasi-geostrophic potential vorticity (QG-PV) transport PDE under the \(\beta\)-plane approximation.
    2. Implement a 2D pseudo-spectral vorticity solver in Python/NumPy conserving total kinetic energy and enstrophy.
    3. Benchmark wave dispersion against analytical theory: \(\omega(k) = U k_x - \frac{\beta k_x}{k_x^2 + k_y^2 + k_R^{-2}}\).
    4. Simulate geostrophic turbulence and spontaneous jet stream formation.
  • Career Impact: Essential methodology for climate modeling, numerical weather prediction, oceanography, and environmental fluid dynamics.

💻 PHYD57H3: Advanced Computational Methods in Physics

Instructor: Prof. Pawel Artymowicz · Key Topics: Linux clusters, OpenMP threading, SIMD vectorization, CUDA GPU architecture, Barnes-Hut, SPH fluids.

Project 1: GPU-Accelerated 3D Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH) with CUDA Shared Memory Tiling

  • The Computational Challenge: SPH simulates fluid dynamics via moving particles. Naive neighbor searches cost \(O(N^2)\), and naive GPU implementations suffer from uncoalesced memory access and warp divergence.
  • Implementation & Deliverables:
    1. Implement a 3D uniform spatial hash grid in CUDA with parallel radix sorting (thrust::sort_by_key) so neighbor particles reside in contiguous GPU memory.
    2. Write custom CUDA kernels that load neighbor cells into GPU shared memory (\(L1\) cache speed) to eliminate memory bandwidth bottlenecks.
    3. Implement Tait’s weakly compressible equation of state and Monaghan artificial viscosity.
    4. Simulate the 3D Rayleigh-Taylor instability for \(N = 2 \times 10^5\) particles in real time, profiling memory bandwidth and warp efficiency using NVIDIA Nsight Compute.
  • Career Impact: Top-tier preparation for GPU kernel engineering, game engine physics (Unreal Engine), visual effects, and high-performance CFD.

Project 2: AVX-512 Hand-Vectorized & OpenMP-Parallelized Barnes-Hut Octree Gravitational Engine

  • The Computational Challenge: Barnes-Hut tree traversals involve irregular pointer chasing that causes CPU cache misses. Maximizing hardware throughput requires cache-oblivious data structures and explicit SIMD vectorization.
  • Implementation & Deliverables:
    1. Implement a Morton-indexed linear octree in C++ stored in flat memory buffers.
    2. Hand-vectorize tree interaction kernels using Intel AVX2/AVX-512 intrinsics (_mm256_fmadd_ps / _mm512_fmadd_ps), computing 8 to 16 particle interactions per instruction.
    3. Parallelize with OpenMP task-based work-stealing for dynamic load balancing.
    4. Construct an empirical Roofline Model using Linux perf to prove execution achieves near-peak hardware compute limits.
  • Career Impact: Core skillset for low-latency quantitative trading (HFT systems architecture) and scientific supercomputing.

📊 STAC67H3: Regression Analysis

Instructor: Prof. Thibault Randrianarisoa · Key Topics: Gauss-Markov theorem, hat matrix, leverage, Cook’s distance, multicollinearity, regularized regression.

Project 1: High-Breakdown Robust Regression & Influence Diagnostics under Heavy-Tailed Heteroskedasticity

  • The Statistical Problem: OLS has an asymptotic breakdown point of \(1/n\)—a single outlier can destroy the model. Furthermore, clustered leverage points cause classical single-case diagnostics (Cook’s distance, DFFITS) to suffer from masking and swamping.
  • Implementation & Deliverables:
    1. Analytically derive influence functions and asymptotic relative efficiency for Huber M-estimators, Theil-Sen estimators, and Rousseeuw’s Least Trimmed Squares (LTS, 50% breakdown point).
    2. Implement Iteratively Reweighted Least Squares (IRLS) from scratch in R, with sandwich covariance estimators for standard errors under heteroskedasticity.
    3. Run 10,000-iteration Monte Carlo breakdown simulations under Cauchy and Student-\(t(2)\) error distributions with clustered high-leverage outliers (\(h_{ii} > 3p/n\)).
    4. Apply to real financial factor models (Fama-French 5-factor across market crashes).
  • Career Impact: Critical in quantitative hedge funds (risk modeling, factor investing where outliers corrupt OLS) and biostatistics.

Project 2: High-Dimensional De-Biased LASSO & Post-Selection Inference (\(p \gg n\))

  • The Statistical Problem: In high-dimensional data (\(p \gg n\)), standard OLS cannot invert \(\mathbf{X}^T\mathbf{X}\). While LASSO performs variable selection, naive \(p\)-values computed after selection suffer from massive post-selection bias (“data snooping”) and false discovery rates near 100%.
  • Implementation & Deliverables:
    1. Implement the De-biased / Desparsified LASSO in R/Python using nodewise regressions to approximate the inverse covariance matrix \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}\).
    2. Mathematically verify asymptotic normality: \(\sqrt{n}(\hat{\beta}_j^{\text{debias}} - \beta_j^*) \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 \Omega_{jj})\).
    3. Construct valid confidence intervals and implement Benjamini-Hochberg False Discovery Rate (FDR) control.
    4. Benchmark on real cancer genomic microarrays (TCGA breast cancer gene expression).
  • Career Impact: Indispensable for biomedical genomics, clinical trial design, and causal machine learning.
