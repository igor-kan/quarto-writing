Executive Summary & Strategic Positioning

Training deep neural networks to approximate complex dynamical systems is increasingly automated. However, neural networks remain opaque black boxes. While an AI can learn to predict turbulent flow or chaotic orbits, only human physicists possess the epistemic framework required to dissect latent activation spaces and extract the analytical invariants discovered by the model.

1. Domain Overview: Mechanistic Interpretability in Physics

Mechanistic Interpretability (MechInterp) reverse-engineers the weights and activations of trained neural networks into human-understandable algorithms and physical laws.

In physics applications, a neural network trained purely on coordinate trajectories often implicitly learns fundamental conservation laws—such as conservation of energy, angular momentum, or adiabatic invariants—to optimize its multi-step prediction loss. MechInterp leverages Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs), activation patching, and probing classifiers to isolate these conserved features from polysemantic superposition.

Why Individual Human Leverage is High (High / Low–Medium Automatability)

  • Epistemic Attribution: AI can optimize loss functions, but it cannot explain why a particular linear combination of latent features corresponds to a Noether invariant. Human domain intuition is indispensable.
  • Small-Model Tractability: You do not need a 70B parameter LLM; profound interpretability research is routinely conducted on compact 3-layer MLPs or 4-layer Transformers trained on classical dynamical systems.

The Primary Bottleneck

  • The Superposition Problem: Individual neurons are polysemantic; a single neuron activates for multiple unrelated physical quantities.
  • Disentangling true causal invariants from spurious statistical correlations in chaotic phase space.

2. Curricular Bridge: Taylor, ASTB23, and PHYD57

  • Taylor Classical Mechanics (Ch. 7):
    • Phase-space flows and canonical energy conservation (\(h = \sum \dot{q}_i p_i - L\)).
    • Coupled non-linear systems: The chaotic double pendulum (7.29) and its phase-space divergence.
  • ASTB23 (Astrophysics: Stars & Galaxies):
    • Stellar dynamics in galactic potentials and Jeans’ Theorem: Any steady-state distribution function \(f(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{v})\) depends on the phase-space coordinates only through the integrals of motion (\(f = f(E, L_z, I_3)\)).
    • The non-analytic “Third Integral” (\(I_3\)) in flattened axisymmetric galactic disks (e.g., Miyamoto–Nagai potentials).
  • PHYD57 (Computational Physics):
    • Symplectic integration (Verlet, leapfrog schemes) preserving phase-space volume (Liouville’s theorem).
    • PyTorch neural network training, latent space extraction, and autoencoder optimization.

3. First Beginner Project (MVP)

  • Goal: Detect whether a neural network trained purely on coordinate trajectories independently discovers conservation of energy.
  • Implementation:
    1. Generate \(10^5\) trajectory steps of a chaotic double pendulum using a symplectic integrator in Python.
    2. Train a 3-layer MLP or small Transformer to predict state \(s_{t+1} = (\theta_1, \theta_2, p_1, p_2)_{t+1}\) from \(s_t\).
    3. Train a Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) on the hidden layer activations to disentangle polysemantic neurons into an overcomplete sparse dictionary: \[\mathbf{a} \approx \sum_{i} c_i \mathbf{f}_i\]
    4. Compute the linear correlation between individual SAE latent features \(c_i\) and the analytical total energy \(E = T + V\). Identify the specific “conservation circuit” inside the network weights.

4. Execution Roadmap

Phase 1: Right Now (Weeks 1–2) — Invariant Discovery in Chaotic Double Pendula

  1. Data Generation: Use a symplectic Verlet integrator to simulate the double pendulum (Taylor 7.29) across diverse energy shells.
  2. Model Training & SAE Disentanglement: Train an MLP to predict \((\theta_1, \theta_2, \dot{\theta}_1, \dot{\theta}_2)_{t+\Delta t}\). Freeze weights, record activations, and train an SAE with an \(L_1\) sparsity penalty:
import torch
import torch.nn as nn

class SparseAutoencoder(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, hidden_dim, dict_dim, l1_coeff=1e-3):
        super().__init__()
        self.encoder = nn.Linear(hidden_dim, dict_dim)
        self.decoder = nn.Linear(dict_dim, hidden_dim)
        self.l1_coeff = l1_coeff

    def forward(self, x):
        # Sparse latent representation
        latents = torch.relu(self.encoder(x))
        reconstruction = self.decoder(latents)
        
        mse_loss = nn.functional.mse_loss(reconstruction, x)
        l1_loss = self.l1_coeff * latents.sum(dim=-1).mean()
        return reconstruction, latents, mse_loss + l1_loss
  1. Causal Circuit Extraction: Compute Pearson correlation between sparse latents \(c_i\) and the analytical Hamiltonian \(H\).
  • Deliverable: A Jupyter notebook isolating the specific latent “energy conservation feature” synthesized by the network.

Phase 2: Near Future (Months 1–2) — Probing the Galactic “Third Integral”

  1. Galactic Trajectory Simulation: In ASTB23, stars orbiting in a flattened Miyamoto–Nagai disk potential conserve energy \(E\) and angular momentum \(L_z\), plus an approximate non-analytic “third integral” \(I_3\) that limits vertical excursion.
  2. Causal Intervention (Activation Patching): Train a Transformer on 3D galactic stellar orbits. Identify candidate \(I_3\) circuits. Systematically ablate or patch these latents and observe whether vertical oscillation stability collapses, proving that the model synthesized Jeans’ distribution function \(f(E, L_z, I_3)\).
  • Deliverable: A technical pre-print mapping internal representations of deep dynamics models directly to galactic stellar orbital theory.
