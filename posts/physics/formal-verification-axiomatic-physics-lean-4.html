Executive Summary & Strategic Positioning

In an era dominated by large language models (LLMs) and automated code generation, scientific domains vary dramatically in their susceptibility to automation. This analysis evaluates research frontiers along two orthogonal axes:

  1. Individual Human Leverage: The ability of an individual researcher—operating without industrial compute clusters or petabyte-scale datasets—to produce durable, mathematically permanent scientific contributions.
  2. AI Automatability: The likelihood of a domain being commoditized by near-term generative models.

1. Domain Overview: Formal Verification & Axiomatic Physics

Formal verification in physics represents the frontier of translating physical theories, dynamical systems, and variational principles into dependent type theory (specifically using the Lean 4 interactive theorem prover).

While pure mathematics has mobilized thousands of contributors to build Mathlib—formalizing algebraic geometry, measure theory, and topology—theoretical physics has historically lacked an axiomatic, computer-checked backbone. Undergraduate textbooks and research literature routinely swap limits, drop boundary variations, assume smooth compact support without verification, and conflate coordinate representations with manifold-independent geometric objects.

Why Individual Human Leverage is Highest (Extremely High)

  • Near-Zero Training Corpus: Neural theorem provers (such as AlphaProof or Lean-Copilot) rely heavily on millions of existing tokens of formalized proofs. Because formalized physics represents less than 0.01% of the formal proof corpus, automated LLM agents cannot effectively guess definitions, tactic sequences, or physical lemmas.
  • Semantic Conceptual Bridging: Connecting physical intuition (e.g., “the trajectory minimizes action”) to foundational measure theory, distribution theory, and jet bundles requires deep mathematical maturity and philosophical clarity that statistical language models fundamentally lack.
  • Permanence: Once a theorem in classical mechanics or thermodynamics is formalized and checked by the Lean 4 kernel, it is mathematically permanent, bug-free, and reusable forever by the global scientific community.

The Primary Bottleneck

The core bottleneck is the profound gap between standard textbook physics and constructive type theory: * Standard physics freely assumes functions \(q(t) \in C^2(\mathbb{R})\), ignores the boundary terms in integration by parts, and invokes infinitesimal variations without specifying the underlying topological function space. * Lean 4 forces explicit proofs of continuity, differentiability, compactness of integration intervals, and rigorous handling of local coordinate charts on tangent bundles.

2. Curricular Bridge: Taylor, ASTB23, and PHYD57

This research agenda directly anchors and deepens the undergraduate physics curriculum:

  • Taylor Classical Mechanics (Ch. 6 & 7):
    • The calculus of variations: formalizing the derivation of Euler–Lagrange equations from stationarity of the action functional \(S[q] = \int L(q, \dot{q}, t) \, dt\).
    • Cyclic coordinates and Noether symmetries: proving that \(\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_i} = 0 \implies \dot{p}_i = 0\) rigorously in dependent type theory.
    • Constrained systems: formalizing holonomic constraints and Lagrange multipliers (\(f(q, t) = 0\)).
  • ASTB23 (Astrophysics: Stars, Galaxies & The Universe):
    • Analytical foundations of stellar structure and gravitational equilibrium: formalizing the classical Virial Theorem (\(2\langle K \rangle + \langle U \rangle = 0\)) and hydrostatic equilibrium \(\frac{dP}{dr} = -\frac{\rho(r) G M(r)}{r^2}\).
  • PHYD57 (Advanced Computational Methods in Physics):
    • Providing verified mathematical specifications against which numerical integrators (symplectic Verlet, Runge–Kutta) can be formally validated.

3. First Beginner Project (MVP)

  • Goal: Formalize a foundational theorem in 1D classical mechanics using Lean 4.
  • Implementation:
    1. Set up a clean Lean 4 project importing Mathlib.
    2. Define a 1D mechanical system with state space \((q, \dot{q}, t) \in \mathbb{R}^3\) and scalar Lagrangian \(L(q, \dot{q}, t) = \frac{1}{2}m\dot{q}^2 - V(q)\).
    3. Prove that if \(V(q)\) is independent of \(q\) (spatial translation invariance), the canonical momentum \(p = \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}} = m\dot{q}\) is an exact invariant along any trajectory satisfying the Euler–Lagrange equation.
  • Next Step: Explore or contribute to emerging community repositories like PhysLib (part of the Lean4PHYS framework) to standardize physical dimensions, units, and basic kinematics.

4. Execution Roadmap

Phase 1: Right Now (Weeks 1–2) — Cyclic Coordinate Invariance in Lean 4

  1. Toolchain Setup: Install Lean 4 via elan and configure a clean package importing Mathlib.
  2. State Space & Lagrangian Definition: Define a generalized 1D mechanical system with state space \((q, \dot{q}, t) \in \mathbb{R}^3\) and a \(C^2\) scalar Lagrangian \(L: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}\).
  3. Euler–Lagrange Predicate: \[\mathrm{EL}(q, L) \iff \frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}}\right) - \frac{\partial L}{\partial q} = 0\]
  4. Statement and Formal Proof of Noether Conservation:
import Mathlib.Analysis.Calculus.Deriv.Basic
import Mathlib.Analysis.Calculus.Deriv.Comp

open Real

-- Predicate stating that a trajectory q : ℝ → ℝ satisfies the 
-- Euler-Lagrange differential equation for Lagrangian L.
def EulerLagrange (L : ℝ → ℝ → ℝ → ℝ) (q : ℝ → ℝ) : Prop :=
  ∀ t, deriv (fun s => (deriv (fun v => L s (q s) v)) (deriv q s)) t - 
       (deriv (fun x => L t x (deriv q t))) (q t) = 0

-- Theorem: If a coordinate is cyclic (∂L/∂q = 0 everywhere), 
-- then conjugate momentum ∂L/∂v is an exact constant of motion.
theorem cyclic_coordinate_conserved 
    (L : ℝ → ℝ → ℝ → ℝ) (q : ℝ → ℝ) 
    (hEL : EulerLagrange L q) 
    (h_cyclic : ∀ t x v, deriv (fun y => L t y v) x = 0) :
    ∀ t, deriv (fun s => deriv (fun v => L s (q s) v) (deriv q s)) t = 0 := by
  intro t
  have hel := hEL t
  have hcyc := h_cyclic t (q t) (deriv q t)
  linarith [hel, hcyc]
  • Deliverable: A self-contained, compiling .lean file demonstrating that generalized momentum conjugate to a cyclic coordinate is an exact constant of motion along valid trajectories.

Phase 2: Near Future (Months 1–2) — Axiomatic Virial Theorem & Constrained Dynamics

  1. Holonomic Constraints: Formalize Taylor §7.7 (Lagrange multipliers for holonomic geometric constraints \(f(\mathbf{q}, t) = 0\)).
  2. The Classical Gravitational Virial Theorem:
    • Define the moment of inertia scalar \(I = \sum_{i} m_i \|\mathbf{r}_i\|^2\).
    • Take its second time derivative: \[\frac{1}{2}\ddot{I} = 2K + \sum_{i} \mathbf{F}_i \cdot \mathbf{r}_i\]
    • For homogeneous power-law potentials \(V \propto r^k\), prove Euler’s homogeneous function theorem in Lean and show that for bound systems with time-average \(\langle \ddot{I} \rangle = 0\), \(2\langle K \rangle + k \langle V \rangle = 0\).
    • Specialize to Newtonian gravitation (\(k = -1\)) to recover \(2\langle K \rangle + \langle V \rangle = 0\).
  • Deliverable: Submit a standalone library or pull request to open-source formal physics initiatives (e.g., PhysLib / Lean4PHYS).

5. Ecosystem, Publication Venues & Dissemination

Open-Source Integration

  • leanprover-community/physlib: The primary official community repository dedicated to digitalizing physics in Lean 4 (formed from merging PhysLean and Lean-QuantumInfo). Target the PhyslibAlpha subdirectory first—it features lighter linting and lower review friction specifically tailored for experimental PRs and student contributions.
  • ShirleyLIYuxin/Lean4PHYS: The framework hosting PhysLib and the LeanPhysBench benchmark. Excellent for contributing undergraduate-level physics theorem statements.
  • leanprover-community/mathlib4: For upstream differential geometry prerequisites (jet bundles, smooth manifolds, tangent bundles).

Communities & Forums

  • Lean Zulip Chat: The premier hub for formal mathematics. Actively participate in the #physics, #mathematics, and #Is there code for X? streams.
