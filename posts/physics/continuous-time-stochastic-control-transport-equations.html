Executive Summary & Strategic Positioning

Modern reinforcement learning and discrete optimization frequently rely on discrete heuristics that fail when dynamic environments undergo severe regime shifts. By contrast, continuous-time stochastic optimal control and kinetic transport equations model reality via rigorous partial differential equations (PDEs)—namely the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman (HJB) equation and the Fokker–Planck equation.

1. Domain Overview: Stochastic Control & Transport Phenomena

This domain combines two foundational continuous-time frameworks:

  1. Optimal Control via the HJB PDE: Minimizing expected cumulative cost functionals in continuous time, generating deterministic feedback controls via viscosity solutions.
  2. Kinetic Transport via the Fokker–Planck Equation: Modeling the statistical evolution of particle distributions subject to deterministic drift and stochastic diffusion: \[\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} = -\nabla \cdot (\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x}) f) + \frac{1}{2} \nabla \nabla : (\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{x}) f)\]

Why Individual Human Leverage is High (High Leverage / Medium Automatability)

  • Analytical Stability Proofs: Viscosity solutions and kinetic conservation laws provide rigorous stability and convergence guarantees that trial-and-error RL cannot match.
  • Vast Cross-Domain Transfer: The mathematical machinery that solves Zermelo’s navigation problem or the Almgren–Chriss execution model translates directly into the astrophysical Fokker–Planck equation governing stellar core collapse in globular clusters.

The Primary Bottleneck

  • Handling non-linear boundary conditions and tracking viscosity solutions when shocks or gradient discontinuities develop.
  • High-dimensional curse of dimensionality when scaling to many continuous state variables.

2. Curricular Bridge: Taylor, ASTB23, and PHYD57

  • Taylor Classical Mechanics (Ch. 6):
    • Zermelo’s Navigation Problem (Problem 6.23): Minimizing aircraft flight time through a linear wind shear \(\mathbf{v}_{\text{wind}} = V y \hat{\mathbf{x}}\). The textbook uses a small-angle perturbation approximation; optimal control solves the exact non-linear Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman PDE.
  • ASTB23 (Astrophysics: Stars & Galaxies):
    • Collisionless Boltzmann Equation (CBE) and Jeans’ equations.
    • Two-Body Gravitational Relaxation & Core Collapse: In dense globular clusters, gravitational encounters drive energy exchange governed by the Fokker–Planck transport equation for \(f(E, t)\), leading to stellar evaporation and core collapse.
  • PHYD57 (Computational Physics & HPC):
    • Numerical PDE solutions: Implicit Crank–Nicolson schemes, upwind differencing, and numerical stability criteria (CFL conditions).
    • High-performance parallelization using OpenMP and SIMD vectorization in C++.

3. First Beginner Project (MVP)

  • Goal: Solve the optimal trade execution problem under non-linear market impact (Almgren–Chriss model).
  • Implementation:
    1. Set up the continuous Almgren–Chriss execution model in Python.
    2. Formulate the state equation for inventory \(q(t)\) subject to temporary and permanent price impact: \[\min \mathbb{E} \int_0^T \left( \gamma \sigma^2 q(t)^2 + \eta \dot{q}(t)^2 \right) dt\]
    3. Solve the resulting Euler–Lagrange boundary value problem analytically to find the hyperbolic trading trajectory \(q(t) = q_0 \frac{\sinh(\kappa(T - t))}{\sinh(\kappa T)}\).
    4. Construct a finite-difference solver to solve the Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman PDE for non-linear impact regimes (\(|\dot{q}|^{3/2}\)) where analytical solutions do not exist.

Phase 2: Near Future (Months 1–2) — High-Performance Fokker–Planck Solver for Stellar Core Collapse

  1. Astrophysical Transport PDE: In ASTB23, globular cluster relaxation is governed by the energy-space Fokker–Planck equation: \[\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} = -\frac{\partial}{\partial E}(\langle \Delta E \rangle f) + \frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial E^2}\left(\langle (\Delta E)^2 \rangle f\right)\]
  2. Implicit Crank–Nicolson Solver: Write a C++ solver with an implicit Crank–Nicolson scheme to guarantee unconditional numerical stability across logarithmic time steps.
  3. OpenMP Parallelization: Parallelize the spatial/energy grid updates (PHYD57 HPC) to simulate stellar evaporation and runaway core collapse, reproducing the self-similar density profile \(\rho(r) \propto r^{-2.23}\).
  • Deliverable: A compiled, parallelized astrophysics simulation engine modeling stellar relaxation and core collapse from first principles.
