1 1. Introduction: Power vs. Energy as Rate vs. Accumulation

In electrical engineering and energy physics, a ubiquitous source of confusion is the conflation of Power and Energy. * Electrical Power \(P(t)\) is the instantaneous rate of energy transfer, measured in Watts (\(\text{W} = \text{Joules / second}\)). * Electrical Energy \(E\) is the total physical work accumulated over a duration of time \([0, T]\), measured in Joules (\(\text{J}\)) or Kilowatt-hours (\(\text{kW}\cdot\text{h} = 3.6 \times 10^6\text{ J}\)).

1.1 1.1 The Fundamental Accumulation Integral

By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus: \[\boxed{E(T) = \int_0^T P(t) \, dt = \int_0^T v(t) \, i(t) \, \cos\phi(t) \, dt}\]

where: * \(v(t)\): instantaneous root-mean-square (RMS) grid voltage (\(120\text{ V}, 230\text{ V}, 400\text{ kV}\)). * \(i(t)\): instantaneous grid current. * \(\cos\phi(t)\): the power factor, accounting for reactive inductive loads (transformers, motors).

Just as the area under an acute viral load curve \(\int_0^{12} f(t) dt\) represents total biological exposure in \((\text{cells/mL})\cdot\text{days}\), the area under an electrical load profile \(\int P(t) dt\) represents total energy consumption in \(\text{GW}\cdot\text{h}\).

2 2. Diurnal Grid Load Dynamics and the “Duck Curve”

Modern power grids must instantaneously balance generation \(P_{\text{gen}}(t)\) and demand \(P_{\text{load}}(t)\) to preserve grid frequency (\(60\text{ Hz} \pm 0.05\text{ Hz}\) in North America; \(50\text{ Hz} \pm 0.05\text{ Hz}\) in Europe): \[\sum P_{\text{gen}}(t) - \sum P_{\text{load}}(t) = 2 \pi J f \frac{df}{dt}\]

2.1 2.1 The Net Load Formulation

With massive deployment of utility-scale and rooftop photovoltaic (PV) solar generation, the grid operator must dispatch thermal, nuclear, and hydroelectric assets to meet the net load:

\[P_{\text{net}}(t) = P_{\text{demand}}(t) - P_{\text{solar}}(t) - P_{\text{wind}}(t)\]

2.2 2.2 The Physics of Solar Insolation

Solar irradiance hitting a horizontal plane \(I_{\text{pv}}(t)\) is governed by the solar zenith angle \(\theta_z(t)\): \[I_{\text{pv}}(t) = I_0 \cdot \tau_{\text{atm}} \max\left(0, \cos\theta_z(t)\right)\]

where \(I_0 \approx 1361\text{ W/m}^2\) is the solar constant, and \(\tau_{\text{atm}} \approx 0.70\) is atmospheric transmittance. Total daily solar energy generated by a solar farm of area \(A_{\text{pv}}\) and conversion efficiency \(\eta \approx 22\%\) is the definite integral: \[E_{\text{solar}} = \eta A_{\text{pv}} \int_{t_{\text{sunrise}}}^{t_{\text{sunset}}} I_{\text{pv}}(t) \, dt\]

2.3 2.3 The Evening Ramp Rate Challenge

Between \(17:00\) and \(20:00\), two opposing gradients collide: 1. Solar irradiance drops precipitously to zero (\(\frac{dP_{\text{solar}}}{dt} \ll 0\)) as the sun sets. 2. Residential electrical demand surges (\(\frac{dP_{\text{demand}}}{dt} > 0\)) as citizens arrive home, activate air conditioning/heating, cook, and charge electric vehicles.

The resulting ramp rate required from dispatchable generation: \[\frac{dP_{\text{net}}}{dt} = \frac{dP_{\text{demand}}}{dt} - \frac{dP_{\text{solar}}}{dt}\]

In the California ISO (CAISO), this evening ramp regularly exceeds \(13,000\text{ MW}\) in under 3 hours (\(> 72\text{ MW/minute}\)). Conventional baseload nuclear and coal stations cannot change power output this rapidly without thermal stress cracking in turbines, requiring fast-ramping aeroderivative gas peakers or multi-gigawatt-hour Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS).

3.1 3.1 First-Principles CMOS Silicon Power Dissipation

At the semiconductor level, an AI accelerator (GPU or TPU) dissipates power via two primary mechanisms:

\[P_{\text{chip}} = P_{\text{dynamic}} + P_{\text{leakage}}\]

  1. Dynamic Switching Power: Arising from the charging and discharging of gate capacitances \(C_{\text{gate}}\) at clock frequency \(f\) and operating voltage \(V_{\text{dd}}\): \[P_{\text{dynamic}} = \alpha C_{\text{gate}} V_{\text{dd}}^2 f\] where \(\alpha\) is the gate switching activity factor.
  2. Static Leakage Power: Subthreshold conduction and gate dielectric tunneling currents \(I_{\text{leak}}\): \[P_{\text{leakage}} = I_{\text{leak}}(T_{\text{junction}}) V_{\text{dd}} \propto T_{\text{junction}}^2 \exp\left(-\frac{q V_{\text{th}}}{k_B T_{\text{junction}}}\right)\] Leakage increases exponentially with silicon junction temperature \(T_{\text{junction}}\), mandating aggressive thermal management.

3.2 3.2 Cluster Sizing: A 100,000 GPU Facility

Consider an enterprise AI training cluster deploying \(100,000\) modern accelerators (e.g., NVIDIA H100 / Blackwell B200 class): * Accelerator Thermal Design Power (TDP): \(P_{\text{gpu}} \approx 700\text{ W} - 1000\text{ W}\) * Host System Overhead: High-performance CPUs, NVLink switches, InfiniBand NICs, optical transceivers, and storage: \(\approx 500\text{ W}\) per 8-GPU node. * Total Server IT Power per GPU: \(P_{\text{IT/gpu}} \approx 1,000\text{ W} = 1.0\text{ kW}\). * Total IT Load: \[P_{\text{IT,cluster}} = 100,000 \times 1.0\text{ kW} = 100,000\text{ kW} = 100\text{ MW}\]

3.3 3.3 The Thermodynamics of Cooling and PUE

All electrical energy consumed by microprocessors is converted into thermal heat via Joule heating (\(\dot{Q} = P_{\text{IT}}\)). To maintain silicon below maximum safe junction temperatures (\(T_{\text{junction}} \le 85^\circ\text{C}\)), this heat must be rejected into the environment via heat exchangers, chillers, and cooling towers.

The energy efficiency of the facility is measured by Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE): \[\boxed{\text{PUE} = \frac{P_{\text{total facility}}}{P_{\text{IT equipment}}} = 1 + \frac{P_{\text{cooling}} + P_{\text{power distribution losses}}}{P_{\text{IT}}}}\]

  • Legacy Air-Cooled Datacenter: \(\text{PUE} \approx 1.40 - 1.60\) (\(40\text{ to }60\text{ MW}\) of additional cooling power for a \(100\text{ MW}\) IT load).
  • State-of-the-Art Liquid-Cooled Facility: Direct-to-chip warm-water cooling (\(\text{PUE} \approx 1.10 - 1.15\)).

For a \(100\text{ MW}\) IT cluster with \(\text{PUE} = 1.15\), the facility draws: \[P_{\text{grid}} = 100\text{ MW} \times 1.15 = 115\text{ MW}\]

3.4 3.4 Energy Integral for Training Foundation Models

Training a frontier dense LLM requires sustained execution over \(t_{\text{train}} \approx 90\text{ days}\) (\(2,160\text{ hours}\)): \[E_{\text{run}} = \int_0^{T_{\text{train}}} P_{\text{grid}}(t) \, dt = 115\text{ MW} \times 2,160\text{ h} = 248,400\text{ MW}\cdot\text{h} \approx 248.4\text{ GW}\cdot\text{h}\]

This single training run consumes as much electrical energy as approximately \(25,000\) average American households consume in an entire year!

4 4. Mathematical Grid Load Model for Numerical Simulation

To quantify grid interactions, we construct a continuous 24-hour diurnal metropolitan load profile \(P(t)\) comprising: 1. Base Residential/Commercial Grid Demand: \[P_{\text{city}}(t) = P_{\text{base}} + A_{\text{morn}} \exp\left(-\frac{(t - 9)^2}{2 \sigma_{\text{morn}}^2}\right) + A_{\text{eve}} \exp\left(-\frac{(t - 19)^2}{2 \sigma_{\text{eve}}^2}\right)\] 2. Solar PV Generation: \[P_{\text{solar}}(t) = P_{\text{solar,peak}} \max\left(0, \sin\left(\frac{\pi (t - 6)}{14}\right)\right)^{1.3} \quad (6 \le t \le 20)\] 3. AI Datacenter Load: \[P_{\text{datacenter}}(t) = P_{\text{dc,base}} + P_{\text{dc,burst}}(t)\] where a \(150\text{ MW}\) cluster operates continuously with scheduled evening training batch surges.

import numpy as np

def sim_power_grid():
    # Time domain: 24 hours
    n_steps = 1440  # 1-minute resolution
    t = np.linspace(0.0, 24.0, n_steps + 1)
    dt = 24.0 / n_steps
    
    # Model Functions [GW]
    def p_city(time):
        base = 4.5
        morn = 2.2 * np.exp(-((time - 9.0)**2) / (2.0 * 2.0**2))
        eve = 3.8 * np.exp(-((time - 19.5)**2) / (2.0 * 2.5**2))
        return base + morn + eve

    def p_solar(time):
        if 6.0 <= time <= 19.5:
            val = np.sin(np.pi * (time - 6.0) / 13.5)
            return 4.2 * (max(0.0, val)**1.3)
        return 0.0

    def p_ai(time):
        # 150 MW base datacenter + 50 MW burst between 14:00 and 22:00
        burst = 0.050 if 14.0 <= time <= 22.0 else 0.0
        return 0.150 + burst

    # Evaluate vectors
    city_vec = np.array([p_city(ti) for ti in t])
    solar_vec = np.array([p_solar(ti) for ti in t])
    ai_vec = np.array([p_ai(ti) for ti in t])
    
    net_vec = city_vec + ai_vec - solar_vec
    
    # Composite Simpson's Integration
    def simpson(vec, h):
        weights = 2.0 * np.ones(len(vec))
        weights[1:-1:2] = 4.0
        weights[0] = 1.0
        weights[-1] = 1.0
        return (h / 3.0) * np.sum(weights * vec)

    e_city = simpson(city_vec, dt)
    e_solar = simpson(solar_vec, dt)
    e_ai = simpson(ai_vec, dt)
    e_net = simpson(net_vec, dt)
    
    # Ramp rate dP_net / dt [MW / hr]
    derivatives = np.gradient(net_vec, dt) * 1000.0  # GW/hr -> MW/hr
    max_ramp = np.max(derivatives[int(15.0 / dt):int(21.0 / dt)])

    print("Python 3 Power Grid & AI Datacenter Simulation:")
    print(f"  Total City Demand:       {e_city:.2f} GWh")
    print(f"  Total Solar Generation:  {e_solar:.2f} GWh")
    print(f"  Total AI Cluster Demand: {e_ai:.2f} GWh ({e_ai * 1000.0:.1f} MWh)")
    print(f"  Net Grid Energy Needed:  {e_net:.2f} GWh")
    print(f"  Peak Net Load:           {np.max(net_vec):.2f} GW")
    print(f"  Max Evening Ramp Rate:   {max_ramp:.2f} MW/hr")

fn p_city(time: f64) -> f64 {
    let base = 4.5;
    let morn = 2.2 * (- (time - 9.0).powi(2) / (2.0 * 4.0)).exp();
    let eve = 3.8 * (- (time - 19.5).powi(2) / (2.0 * 6.25)).exp();

}

fn p_solar(time: f64) -> f64 {
    if (6.0..=19.5).contains(&time) {
        let val = (PI * (time - 6.0) / 13.5).sin();
        if val > 0.0 { 4.2 * val.powf(1.3) } else { 0.0 }
    } else {
        0.0
    }
}

fn p_ai(time: f64) -> f64 {
    let burst = if (14.0..=22.0).contains(&time) { 0.050 } else { 0.0 };
    0.150 + burst
}

fn simpson(v: &[f64], dt: f64) -> f64 {
    let n = v.len() - 1;
    let mut sum = v[0] + v[n];
    for i in 1..n {
        sum += if i % 2 == 1 { 4.0 } else { 2.0 } * v[i];
    }
    sum * (dt / 3.0)
}

## 1. Introduction: Power vs. Energy as Rate vs. Accumulation

In electrical engineering and energy physics, a ubiquitous source of confusion is the conflation of **Power** and **Energy**. 
* **Electrical Power** $P(t)$ is the instantaneous rate of energy transfer, measured in **Watts** ($\text{W} = \text{Joules / second}$).
* **Electrical Energy** $E$ is the total physical work accumulated over a duration of time $[0, T]$, measured in **Joules** ($\text{J}$) or **Kilowatt-hours** ($\text{kW}\cdot\text{h} = 3.6 \times 10^6\text{ J}$).

```

   6  |   Base|       '--'             \   <-- Total Energy E = Integral P(t) dt
   4  |   Load|                         \
      |-------+--------------------------\----->
   0  +-------+-------+-------+----------+-----> Time t [hours]
      0       6      12      18         24
```

### 1.1 The Fundamental Accumulation Integral

By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus:
$$\boxed{E(T) = \int_0^T P(t) \, dt = \int_0^T v(t) \, i(t) \, \cos\phi(t) \, dt}$$

where:
* $v(t)$: instantaneous root-mean-square (RMS) grid voltage ($120\text{ V}, 230\text{ V}, 400\text{ kV}$).
* $i(t)$: instantaneous grid current.
* $\cos\phi(t)$: the power factor, accounting for reactive inductive loads (transformers, motors).

Just as the area under an acute viral load curve $\int_0^{12} f(t) dt$ represents total biological exposure in $(\text{cells/mL})\cdot\text{days}$, the area under an electrical load profile $\int P(t) dt$ represents total energy consumption in $\text{GW}\cdot\text{h}$.

## 2. Diurnal Grid Load Dynamics and the "Duck Curve"

Modern power grids must instantaneously balance generation $P_{\text{gen}}(t)$ and demand $P_{\text{load}}(t)$ to preserve grid frequency ($60\text{ Hz} \pm 0.05\text{ Hz}$ in North America; $50\text{ Hz} \pm 0.05\text{ Hz}$ in Europe):
$$\sum P_{\text{gen}}(t) - \sum P_{\text{load}}(t) = 2 \pi J f \frac{df}{dt}$$

### 2.1 The Net Load Formulation

With massive deployment of utility-scale and rooftop photovoltaic (PV) solar generation, the grid operator must dispatch thermal, nuclear, and hydroelectric assets to meet the **net load**:

$$P_{\text{net}}(t) = P_{\text{demand}}(t) - P_{\text{solar}}(t) - P_{\text{wind}}(t)$$

### 2.2 The Physics of Solar Insolation

Solar irradiance hitting a horizontal plane $I_{\text{pv}}(t)$ is governed by the solar zenith angle $\theta_z(t)$:
$$I_{\text{pv}}(t) = I_0 \cdot \tau_{\text{atm}} \max\left(0, \cos\theta_z(t)\right)$$

where $I_0 \approx 1361\text{ W/m}^2$ is the solar constant, and $\tau_{\text{atm}} \approx 0.70$ is atmospheric transmittance. Total daily solar energy generated by a solar farm of area $A_{\text{pv}}$ and conversion efficiency $\eta \approx 22\%$ is the definite integral:
$$E_{\text{solar}} = \eta A_{\text{pv}} \int_{t_{\text{sunrise}}}^{t_{\text{sunset}}} I_{\text{pv}}(t) \, dt$$

### 2.3 The Evening Ramp Rate Challenge

Between $17:00$ and $20:00$, two opposing gradients collide:
1. Solar irradiance drops precipitously to zero ($\frac{dP_{\text{solar}}}{dt} \ll 0$) as the sun sets.
2. Residential electrical demand surges ($\frac{dP_{\text{demand}}}{dt} > 0$) as citizens arrive home, activate air conditioning/heating, cook, and charge electric vehicles.

The resulting ramp rate required from dispatchable generation:
$$\frac{dP_{\text{net}}}{dt} = \frac{dP_{\text{demand}}}{dt} - \frac{dP_{\text{solar}}}{dt}$$

In the California ISO (CAISO), this evening ramp regularly exceeds **$13,000\text{ MW}$ in under 3 hours** ($> 72\text{ MW/minute}$). Conventional baseload nuclear and coal stations cannot change power output this rapidly without thermal stress cracking in turbines, requiring fast-ramping aeroderivative gas peakers or multi-gigawatt-hour Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS).

### 3.1 First-Principles CMOS Silicon Power Dissipation

At the semiconductor level, an AI accelerator (GPU or TPU) dissipates power via two primary mechanisms:

$$P_{\text{chip}} = P_{\text{dynamic}} + P_{\text{leakage}}$$

1. **Dynamic Switching Power**: Arising from the charging and discharging of gate capacitances $C_{\text{gate}}$ at clock frequency $f$ and operating voltage $V_{\text{dd}}$:
   $$P_{\text{dynamic}} = \alpha C_{\text{gate}} V_{\text{dd}}^2 f$$
   where $\alpha$ is the gate switching activity factor.
2. **Static Leakage Power**: Subthreshold conduction and gate dielectric tunneling currents $I_{\text{leak}}$:
   $$P_{\text{leakage}} = I_{\text{leak}}(T_{\text{junction}}) V_{\text{dd}} \propto T_{\text{junction}}^2 \exp\left(-\frac{q V_{\text{th}}}{k_B T_{\text{junction}}}\right)$$
   Leakage increases exponentially with silicon junction temperature $T_{\text{junction}}$, mandating aggressive thermal management.

### 3.2 Cluster Sizing: A 100,000 GPU Facility

Consider an enterprise AI training cluster deploying $100,000$ modern accelerators (e.g., NVIDIA H100 / Blackwell B200 class):
* Accelerator Thermal Design Power (TDP): $P_{\text{gpu}} \approx 700\text{ W} - 1000\text{ W}$
* Host System Overhead: High-performance CPUs, NVLink switches, InfiniBand NICs, optical transceivers, and storage: $\approx 500\text{ W}$ per 8-GPU node.
* Total Server IT Power per GPU: $P_{\text{IT/gpu}} \approx 1,000\text{ W} = 1.0\text{ kW}$.
* Total IT Load:
  $$P_{\text{IT,cluster}} = 100,000 \times 1.0\text{ kW} = 100,000\text{ kW} = 100\text{ MW}$$

### 3.3 The Thermodynamics of Cooling and PUE

All electrical energy consumed by microprocessors is converted into thermal heat via Joule heating ($\dot{Q} = P_{\text{IT}}$). To maintain silicon below maximum safe junction temperatures ($T_{\text{junction}} \le 85^\circ\text{C}$), this heat must be rejected into the environment via heat exchangers, chillers, and cooling towers.

The energy efficiency of the facility is measured by **Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE)**:
$$\boxed{\text{PUE} = \frac{P_{\text{total facility}}}{P_{\text{IT equipment}}} = 1 + \frac{P_{\text{cooling}} + P_{\text{power distribution losses}}}{P_{\text{IT}}}}$$

* **Legacy Air-Cooled Datacenter**: $\text{PUE} \approx 1.40 - 1.60$ ($40\text{ to }60\text{ MW}$ of additional cooling power for a $100\text{ MW}$ IT load).
* **State-of-the-Art Liquid-Cooled Facility**: Direct-to-chip warm-water cooling ($\text{PUE} \approx 1.10 - 1.15$).

For a $100\text{ MW}$ IT cluster with $\text{PUE} = 1.15$, the facility draws:
$$P_{\text{grid}} = 100\text{ MW} \times 1.15 = 115\text{ MW}$$

### 3.4 Energy Integral for Training Foundation Models

Training a frontier dense LLM requires sustained execution over $t_{\text{train}} \approx 90\text{ days}$ ($2,160\text{ hours}$):
$$E_{\text{run}} = \int_0^{T_{\text{train}}} P_{\text{grid}}(t) \, dt = 115\text{ MW} \times 2,160\text{ h} = 248,400\text{ MW}\cdot\text{h} \approx 248.4\text{ GW}\cdot\text{h}$$

This single training run consumes as much electrical energy as approximately $25,000$ average American households consume in an entire year!

```python
import numpy as np

def sim_power_grid():
    # Time domain: 24 hours
    n_steps = 1440  # 1-minute resolution
    t = np.linspace(0.0, 24.0, n_steps + 1)
    dt = 24.0 / n_steps
    
    # Model Functions [GW]
    def p_city(time):
        base = 4.5
        morn = 2.2 * np.exp(-((time - 9.0)**2) / (2.0 * 2.0**2))
        eve = 3.8 * np.exp(-((time - 19.5)**2) / (2.0 * 2.5**2))
        return base + morn + eve

    def p_solar(time):
        if 6.0 <= time <= 19.5:
            val = np.sin(np.pi * (time - 6.0) / 13.5)
            return 4.2 * (max(0.0, val)**1.3)
        return 0.0

    def p_ai(time):
        # 150 MW base datacenter + 50 MW burst between 14:00 and 22:00
        burst = 0.050 if 14.0 <= time <= 22.0 else 0.0
        return 0.150 + burst

    # Evaluate vectors
    city_vec = np.array([p_city(ti) for ti in t])
    solar_vec = np.array([p_solar(ti) for ti in t])
    ai_vec = np.array([p_ai(ti) for ti in t])
    
    net_vec = city_vec + ai_vec - solar_vec
    
    # Composite Simpson's Integration
    def simpson(vec, h):
        weights = 2.0 * np.ones(len(vec))
        weights[1:-1:2] = 4.0
        weights[0] = 1.0
        weights[-1] = 1.0
        return (h / 3.0) * np.sum(weights * vec)

    e_city = simpson(city_vec, dt)
    e_solar = simpson(solar_vec, dt)
    e_ai = simpson(ai_vec, dt)
    e_net = simpson(net_vec, dt)
    
    # Ramp rate dP_net / dt [MW / hr]
    derivatives = np.gradient(net_vec, dt) * 1000.0  # GW/hr -> MW/hr
    max_ramp = np.max(derivatives[int(15.0 / dt):int(21.0 / dt)])

    print("Python 3 Power Grid & AI Datacenter Simulation:")
    print(f"  Total City Demand:       {e_city:.2f} GWh")
    print(f"  Total Solar Generation:  {e_solar:.2f} GWh")
    print(f"  Total AI Cluster Demand: {e_ai:.2f} GWh ({e_ai * 1000.0:.1f} MWh)")
    print(f"  Net Grid Energy Needed:  {e_net:.2f} GWh")
    print(f"  Peak Net Load:           {np.max(net_vec):.2f} GW")
    print(f"  Max Evening Ramp Rate:   {max_ramp:.2f} MW/hr")
```

```rust
use std::f64::consts::PI;

fn p_city(time: f64) -> f64 {
    let base = 4.5;
    let morn = 2.2 * (- (time - 9.0).powi(2) / (2.0 * 4.0)).exp();
    let eve = 3.8 * (- (time - 19.5).powi(2) / (2.0 * 6.25)).exp();
    base + morn + eve
}

fn p_solar(time: f64) -> f64 {
    if (6.0..=19.5).contains(&time) {
        let val = (PI * (time - 6.0) / 13.5).sin();
        if val > 0.0 { 4.2 * val.powf(1.3) } else { 0.0 }
    } else {
        0.0
    }
}

fn p_ai(time: f64) -> f64 {
    let burst = if (14.0..=22.0).contains(&time) { 0.050 } else { 0.0 };
    0.150 + burst
}

fn simpson(v: &[f64], dt: f64) -> f64 {
    let n = v.len() - 1;
    let mut sum = v[0] + v[n];
    for i in 1..n {
        sum += if i % 2 == 1 { 4.0 } else { 2.0 } * v[i];
    }
    sum * (dt / 3.0)
}

fn main() {
    let n_steps = 1440;
    let dt = 24.0 / (n_steps as f64);

    let mut city_vec = Vec::with_capacity(n_steps + 1);
    let mut solar_vec = Vec::with_capacity(n_steps + 1);
    let mut ai_vec = Vec::with_capacity(n_steps + 1);
    let mut net_vec = Vec::with_capacity(n_steps + 1);

    for i in 0..=n_steps {
        let t = (i as f64) * dt;
        let c = p_city(t);
        let s = p_solar(t);
        let a = p_ai(t);
        city_vec.push(c);
        solar_vec.push(s);
        ai_vec.push(a);
        net_vec.push(c + a - s);
    }

    let e_city = simpson(&city_vec, dt);
    let e_solar = simpson(&solar_vec, dt);
    let e_ai = simpson(&ai_vec, dt);
    let e_net = simpson(&net_vec, dt);

    let max_net = net_vec.iter().cloned().fold(f64::NEG_INFINITY, f64::max);

    let idx_start = (15.0 / dt) as usize;
    let idx_end = (21.0 / dt) as usize;
    let mut max_ramp = 0.0_f64;
    for i in idx_start..idx_end {
        let ramp = (net_vec[i + 1] - net_vec[i]) / dt * 1000.0;
```
