Embedded Riemannian Surface (\(\mathcal{M} \subset \mathbb{R}^3\))

Submanifold Embedding: A 2-dimensional surface \(\mathcal{M}\) defined inside ambient 3D Euclidean space \(\mathbb{R}^3\) by an injective immersion \(\iota: \mathcal{M} \hookrightarrow \mathbb{R}^3\) that is a topological embedding.

Induced Metric (First Fundamental Form): The metric on \(\mathcal{M}\) is not invented independently; it is inherited (pulled back) directly from the dot product of \(\mathbb{R}^3\): \[ g(v, w) = \iota^*(\delta)(v, w) = d\iota(v) \cdot d\iota(w) \quad \forall v, w \in T_p \mathcal{M} \]

The Area Functional

Generate a surface of revolution by rotating a planar profile curve \(y = y(x)\) around the \(x\)-axis.

Consider an infinitesimal segment of the curve of length \(ds = \sqrt{dx^2 + dy^2} = \sqrt{1 + y'^2}\,dx\). As this segment rotates around the \(x\)-axis at distance \(y(x)\), it sweeps out a narrow cylindrical ribbon of radius \(y\) and width \(ds\).

The surface area of this ribbon is its circumference times its slant width: \[ dA = 2\pi y \, ds = 2\pi y \sqrt{1 + y'^2} \, dx \]

Integrating from boundary ring \(x_1\) to \(x_2\) yields the total area functional: \[ A[y] = 2\pi \int_{x_1}^{x_2} y(x) \sqrt{1 + y'(x)^2} \, dx \]

Axisymmetry

Definition: Continuous rotational symmetry about a straight axis (\(SO(2)\) Lie group invariance).

Consequence in Variational Calculus: The area integrand \(f(y, y') = y\sqrt{1 + y'^2}\) does not contain the independent variable \(x\) explicitly (\(\partial f / \partial x = 0\)).

By Noether’s theorem, this translational symmetry along the symmetry axis generates a first integral of motion—the Beltrami identity: \[ f - y'\frac{\partial f}{\partial y'} = C \implies \frac{y}{\sqrt{1 + y'^2}} = C \equiv y_0 \]

Rearranging and integrating yields the profile curve of the catenoid: \[ y(x) = y_0 \cosh\left(\frac{x - x_0}{y_0}\right) \]

Zero Mean Curvature (\(H = 0\)) vs. Gaussian Curvature (\(K\))

The Two Principal Curvatures: At any point on a 2D surface in \(\mathbb{R}^3\), slice the surface with normal planes to find the directions of maximum and minimum bending, yielding principal curvatures \(\kappa_1\) and \(\kappa_2\).

Gaussian Curvature (\(K\)): The intrinsic product: \[ K = \kappa_1 \kappa_2 \] For the catenoid \(y = y_0 \cosh(x/y_0)\), the principal curvatures are equal in magnitude and opposite in sign: \[ \kappa_1 = \frac{1}{y_0 \cosh^2(x/y_0)}, \quad \kappa_2 = -\frac{1}{y_0 \cosh^2(x/y_0)} \] Therefore, Gaussian curvature is strictly negative everywhere (\(K < 0\)): the surface is a saddle at every point.

Mean Curvature (\(H\)): The extrinsic average: \[ H = \frac{1}{2}(\kappa_1 + \kappa_2) \]

Why \(H = 0\) Defines a Minimal Surface

By the first variation of area formula, if you perturb an embedded surface along its unit normal vector field \(\mathbf{n}\) with test function \(\eta\): \[ \delta A = -2 \iint_{\mathcal{M}} H \, \eta \, dA \] For the area functional to be stationary (\(\delta A = 0\)) for all arbitrary variations \(\eta\), the mean curvature must vanish identically: \[ H \equiv 0 \iff \kappa_1 = -\kappa_2 \]

The Physical Soap-Film Connection (Young–Laplace Equation)

A soap film suspended between two wire rings balances pressure across its membrane according to the Young-Laplace equation: \[ \Delta P = 2 \gamma H \] where \(\gamma\) is surface tension and \(\Delta P\) is the pressure difference between the inside and outside.

Because both sides of the film are exposed to the same atmospheric pressure, \(\Delta P = 0\). Since \(\gamma > 0\), physical equilibrium forces: \[ H = 0 \]

The soap film naturally minimizes its surface energy functional \(U = \gamma A[y]\) by adopting a shape with zero mean curvature—the catenoid.
