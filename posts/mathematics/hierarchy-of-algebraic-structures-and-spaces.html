“Mathematics is the study of structures, and mathematical structures form a hierarchy ordered by the axioms they satisfy.”
— A structuralist motto

Every branch of mathematics studies sets equipped with structure. Add a binary operation, get a magma; add associativity, a semigroup; add an identity, a monoid; add inverses, a group; add a second operation, a ring; add inverses to the second operation, a field. In parallel, add “closeness,” get a topology; add a distance, a metric space; add a norm, a normed space; add an inner product, a Hilbert space. This essay draws the complete map, with a single all-encompassing flowchart, and verifies the axioms computationally.

1 1. Structures as Signatures Plus Axioms

Definition 1 (Definition 1.1 (Mathematical structure)) A structure is a tuple \((X, \sigma_1, \sigma_2, \dots)\) where \(X\) is a set (or class) and the \(\sigma_i\) are operations, relations, or distinguished elements, subject to axioms expressed in first-order logic. A structure is weaker than another if it satisfies fewer axioms; the collection of structures of a given signature ordered this way forms a lattice.

The method of the whole subject: start minimal, add one axiom at a time, and watch the theory become richer. The diagrams below are the result.

2 2. The Algebraic Ladder

2.1 2.1 Magmas Through Groups

Structure Signature Axioms Canonical example
Magma (groupoid) \(({}\cdot{})\) closure only \((\mathbb{Z},-)\)
Semigroup \(({}\cdot{})\) associative positive integers under \(+\)
Monoid \(({}\cdot{},e)\) assoc. + identity \((\mathbb{N},+,0)\)
Group \(({}\cdot,{}^{-1},e)\) monoid + inverses \((\mathbb{Z},+)\), \(S_n\)
Abelian group same group + commutative \((\mathbb{Z},+)\), \((\mathbb{Q},+)\)
Quasigroup \(({}\cdot,/, \backslash)\) division defined loop theory
Loop quasigroup + \(e\) + identity non-associative groups

Dropping commutativity of addition moves from abelian groups to general groups (e.g. the quaternion group \(Q_8\), symmetric groups \(S_n\) for \(n\ge3\)).

2.2 2.2 Rings Through Fields

Structure Extra axiom Example
Ring (rng) two operations, distributive \(2\mathbb{Z}\) (no \(1\))
Ring with unity multiplicative identity \(1\) \(\mathbb{Z}\)
Commutative ring \(ab=ba\) \(\mathbb{Z}[x]\)
Integral domain no zero divisors \(\mathbb{Z}\), \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\)
UFD unique factorization into irreducibles \(\mathbb{Z}[x]\)
PID every ideal is principal \(\mathbb{Z}\), \(k[x]\)
Euclidean domain division algorithm \(\mathbb{Z}\), \(\mathbb{Z}[i]\)
Field every nonzero element invertible \(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{F}_p\)

The chain is strict: \[ \text{fields} \subsetneq \text{Euclidean domains} \subsetneq \text{PIDs} \subsetneq \text{UFDs} \subsetneq \text{integral domains} \subsetneq \text{commutative rings}. \] For example \(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]\) is an integral domain but not a UFD (\(6=2\cdot3=(1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5})\)).

2.3 2.3 Modules, Vector Spaces, and Algebras

  • Module over a ring \(R\): an abelian group \(M\) with a scalar action \(R\times M\to M\) satisfying the module axioms. \(R=\mathbb{Z}\) recovers abelian groups; \(R\) a field gives a vector space.
  • Vector space over a field \(k\): a module over a field; every vector space has a basis (uses the Axiom of Choice in general), with a well-defined dimension.
  • Algebra over a ring: a ring that is also a module, with a compatible multiplication (e.g. matrix algebras, group algebras, Lie algebras).

2.4 2.4 Lattices and Boolean Algebras

Structure Axioms Example
Partial order reflexive, antisym., transitive \((\mathcal{P}(S),\subseteq)\)
Lattice every pair has meet \(\wedge\) and join \(\vee\) divisors of \(n\) under gcd/lcm
Distributive lattice \(a\wedge(b\vee c)=(a\wedge b)\vee(a\wedge c)\) Boolean algebras
Boolean algebra distributive + complements \(\mathcal{P}(S)\) with \(\cap,\cup,{}^c\)
Heyting algebra distributive + relative pseudo-complement open sets of a topology

Boolean algebras model propositional logic and digital circuits; Heyting algebras model intuitionistic logic (dropping the law of excluded middle — connecting to the constructivist discussion).

3 3. Categories, Functors, and Monads

Definition 2 (Definition 3.1 (Category)) A category \(\mathcal{C}\) consists of objects \(\operatorname{ob}\mathcal{C}\), morphisms \(\hom(A,B)\) for each pair, a composition \(\circ:\hom(B,C)\times\hom(A,B)\to\hom(A,C)\), and identity morphisms \(\mathrm{id}_A\), satisfying: \[ (h\circ g)\circ f=h\circ(g\circ f),\qquad \mathrm{id}_B\circ f=f=f\circ \mathrm{id}_A. \]

  • A monoid is exactly a one-object category (elements = morphisms, \(e=\mathrm{id}\)).
  • A groupoid is a category in which every morphism is invertible (a group is a one-object groupoid).
  • A functor preserves objects, morphisms, identities, and composition.
  • A natural transformation compares functors.
  • A monad is an endofunctor \(T\) with natural transformations \(\eta:\mathrm{Id}\to T\) (unit) and \(\mu:T^2\to T\) (join) satisfying the monad laws (\(\mu\circ T\mu=\mu\circ\mu T\), \(\mu\circ \eta T=\mathrm{id}=\mu\circ T\eta\)). Monads are the categorical theory of effects (nondeterminism, state, exceptions, IO), central to functional programming (Haskell) and to algebraic theories (Eilenberg–Moore algebras).

This is the precise sense in which the user’s chain “magmas … monads” lives on two axes at once: algebraic (groups, rings, fields) and categorical (monoid = one-object category; monad = monoid in the category of endofunctors).

4 4. The Spatial Ladder

Definition 3 (Ladders of Spaces) \[ \begin{aligned} &\textbf{Set} \to \textbf{Topological space} \to \textbf{Metric space} \to \textbf{Normed space} \to \textbf{Inner product space} \to \textbf{Hilbert space},\\ &\textbf{Metric space} \to \textbf{Uniform space} \to \textbf{Topological space} \quad(\text{reversed}),\\ &\textbf{Measurable space} \to \textbf{Measure space} \to \textbf{Probability space},\\ &\textbf{Topological manifold} \to \textbf{Smooth manifold} \to \textbf{Riemannian manifold} \to \textbf{Lorentzian manifold}. \end{aligned} \]

Space Added structure Key theorem
Set none cardinality
Topological space open sets continuity, compactness, connectedness
Metric space distance completion, Baire, Heine–Borel
Normed space norm \(\lVert x\rVert\) triangle inequality, metric from norm
Inner product space \(\langle x,y\rangle\) Cauchy–Schwarz, orthogonality, projections
Hilbert space complete inner product Riesz representation, spectral theorem
Banach space complete normed Banach fixed point, open mapping
Measurable space \(\sigma\)-algebra measurability
Measure space measure \(\mu\) Lebesgue integration, monotone/Fatou/dominated convergence
Probability space measure with \(\mu(\Omega)=1\) LLN, CLT
Manifold locally Euclidean topology charts, tangent spaces, Stokes’ theorem

Every normed space is metric (\(d(x,y)=\lVert x-y\rVert\)); every inner product space is normed (\(\lVert x\rVert=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle}\)); every metric space is topological (open balls); not conversely — the cofinite topology is non-metrizable, and \(\ell^p\) for \(0<p<1\) is a metric space that is not normable.

8 8. Computational Laboratory

The lab verifies the axioms of finite groups, monoids, rings, fields, vector spaces, lattices, Boolean algebras, a small category, and the list monad.

Code 
import itertools
from functools import reduce

print("=== 1. FINITE GROUP: (Z_n, +) AND (Z_p*, ×) ===")
def check_group(elements, op, e):
    S = set(elements)
    closed = all(op(a, b) in S for a in S for b in S)
    assoc = all(op(op(a, b), c) == op(a, op(b, c)) for a in S for b in S for c in S)
    ident = all(op(a, e) == a and op(e, a) == a for a in S)
    invs = all(any(op(a, b) == e and op(b, a) == e for b in S) for a in S)
    return closed, assoc, ident, invs

for n in [4, 5, 6]:
    Z = list(range(n))
    print(f"  (Z_{n}, +):  closed={check_group(Z, lambda a,b:(a+b)%n, 0)}")

print("\n  (Z_p*, ×) is a group iff p prime:")
for p in [5, 6, 7]:
    Zp = [a for a in range(1, p)]
    print(f"    p={p}: {check_group(Zp, lambda a,b:(a*b)%p, 1)}  (field iff all inverses)")

print("\n=== 2. MONOID vs GROUP ===")
N = list(range(0, 6))
print("  (N, +, 0) monoid axioms:",
      "assoc", all((a+b)+c == a+(b+c) for a in N for b in N for c in N),
      "| identity", all(0+a == a for a in N),
      "| inverses for all?", all(any(a+b == 0 for b in N) for a in N), "(false => not a group)")

print("\n=== 3. RING vs FIELD: Z_n UNDER + AND × ===")
def is_field(n):
    if n < 2: return False
    S = list(range(n))
    add_closed = all((a+b) % n in S for a in S for b in S)
    mul_assoc = all((a*b)*c % n == a*(b*c) % n for a in S for b in S for c in S)
    distrib = all(a*(b+c) % n == (a*b + a*c) % n for a in S for b in S for c in S)
    inv = all(any((a*b) % n == 1 for b in S) for a in S if a != 0)
    return add_closed and mul_assoc and distrib and inv
for n in [4, 5, 6, 7, 11]:
    print(f"  Z_{n} is a field: {is_field(n)}  (prime => True)")

print("\n=== 4. VECTOR SPACE AXIOMS IN R^2 ===")
import random
random.seed(0)
def add(u, v): return (u[0]+v[0], u[1]+v[1])
def smul(c, u): return (c*u[0], c*u[1])
vecs = [(random.randint(-9, 9), random.randint(-9, 9)) for _ in range(50)]
scalars = [random.randint(-5, 5) for _ in range(20)]   # integers => exact arithmetic
assoc_add = all(add(add(u,v),w) == add(u,add(v,w)) for u in vecs for v in vecs for w in vecs)
comm_add = all(add(u,v) == add(v,u) for u in vecs for v in vecs)
zero = (0,0)
ident = all(add(u, zero) == u for u in vecs)
distrib1 = all(smul(c, add(u,v)) == add(smul(c,u), smul(c,v)) for c in scalars for u in vecs for v in vecs)
distrib2 = all(smul(c+d, u) == add(smul(c,u), smul(d,u)) for c in scalars for d in scalars for u in vecs)
print(f"  associativity={assoc_add}, commutativity={comm_add}, zero={ident}, distributivity={distrib1 and distrib2}")

print("\n=== 5. LATTICE: DIVISORS OF 12 UNDER gcd/lcm ===")
from math import gcd
def lcm(a, b): return a*b // gcd(a, b)
divs = [d for d in range(1, 13) if 12 % d == 0]
meet_join = all(gcd(a,b) in divs and lcm(a,b) in divs for a in divs for b in divs)
distrib = all(gcd(a, lcm(b,c)) == lcm(gcd(a,b), gcd(a,c)) for a in divs for b in divs for c in divs)
print(f"  divisors={divs}; closed under meet/join={meet_join}; distributive={distrib}")

print("\n=== 6. BOOLEAN ALGEBRA: POWER SET OF {1,2,3} ===")
U = {1,2,3}
Power = [frozenset(s) for r in range(4) for s in itertools.combinations(U, r)]
def comp(A): return frozenset(U - A)
distrib = all((A & (B | C)) == ((A & B) | (A & C)) for A in Power for B in Power for C in Power)
complement = all((A | comp(A)) == frozenset(U) and (A & comp(A)) == frozenset() for A in Power)
print(f"  |P(U)|={len(Power)}; distributive={distrib}; complements={complement}")

print("\n=== 7. SMALL CATEGORY: IDENTITY AND ASSOCIATIVITY ===")
# Objects A, B; morphisms idA, idB, f: A -> B.  Represent a morphism by (domain, codomain).
morph = {'idA': ('A', 'A'), 'idB': ('B', 'B'), 'f': ('A', 'B')}

def compose(g, h):
    """Return g ∘ h (apply h first): h: X->Y, g: Y->Z."""
    if h[1] != g[0]:
        return None
    if h in (morph['idA'], morph['idB']):
        return g
    if g in (morph['idA'], morph['idB']):
        return h
    return (h[0], g[1])

ids_ok = True
for name, (X, Y) in morph.items():
    idX = ('A', 'A') if X == 'A' else ('B', 'B')
    idY = ('A', 'A') if Y == 'A' else ('B', 'B')
    left = compose(idY, morph[name])    # id_Y ∘ m
    right = compose(morph[name], idX)   # m ∘ id_X
    print(f"  {name} ({X}->{Y}): id_Y∘{name}={left}, {name}∘id_X={right}")
    if left != morph[name] or right != morph[name]:
        ids_ok = False
print(f"  identity laws hold: {ids_ok}")
print(f"  composition f ∘ idA = {compose(morph['f'], morph['idA'])}")

print("\n=== 8. THE LIST MONAD: UNIT AND BIND LAWS ===")
unit = lambda x: [x]
def bind(xs, f): return [y for x in xs for y in f(x)]
f = lambda x: [x, x+1]
g = lambda x: [x*10]
m = [1, 2, 3]
left_id  = bind(unit(7), f) == f(7)
right_id = bind(m, unit) == m
assoc    = bind(bind(m, f), g) == bind(m, lambda x: bind(f(x), g))
print(f"  left identity  (unit a >>= f = f a): {left_id}")
print(f"  right identity (m >>= unit = m):     {right_id}")
print(f"  associativity  (m >>= f) >>= g:      {assoc}")
print("  => List is a monad: the categorical structure behind nondeterminism.")

9 9. Summary

\[ \begin{aligned} &\textbf{Algebra: } && \text{magma}\to\text{semigroup}\to\text{monoid}\to\text{group}\to\text{ring}\to\text{field};\\ & && \text{module}\to\text{vector space};\ \text{partial order}\to\text{lattice}\to\text{Boolean algebra}.\\[3pt] &\textbf{Categories: } && \text{monoid}=\text{one-object category};\ \text{group}=\text{one-object groupoid};\ \text{monad}=\text{monoid of endofunctors}.\\[3pt] &\textbf{Spaces: } && \text{Set}\to\text{Top}\to\text{Metric}\to\text{Normed}\to\text{Inner}\to\text{Hilbert};\ \ \text{Measure}\to\text{Probability}.\\[3pt] &\textbf{Hybrids: } && \text{topological/Lie groups, Banach/C*-algebras, Riemannian manifolds.} \end{aligned} \]

The hierarchy is the periodic table of mathematics: each structure is a setting in which certain theorems become true, and the functors between them translate results from one setting to another.

| Structure | Signature | Axioms | Canonical example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Magma** (groupoid) | $({}\cdot{})$ | closure only | $(\mathbb{Z},-)$ |
| **Semigroup** | $({}\cdot{})$ | associative | positive integers under $+$ |
| **Monoid** | $({}\cdot{},e)$ | assoc. + identity | $(\mathbb{N},+,0)$ |
| **Group** | $({}\cdot,{}^{-1},e)$ | monoid + inverses | $(\mathbb{Z},+)$, $S_n$ |
| **Abelian group** | same | group + commutative | $(\mathbb{Z},+)$, $(\mathbb{Q},+)$ |
| **Quasigroup** | $({}\cdot,/, \backslash)$ | division defined | loop theory |
| **Loop** | quasigroup + $e$ | + identity | non-associative groups |

Dropping commutativity of addition moves from abelian groups to general groups (e.g. the quaternion group $Q_8$, symmetric groups $S_n$ for $n\ge3$).

### 2.2 Rings Through Fields

| Structure | Extra axiom | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ring (rng)** | two operations, distributive | $2\mathbb{Z}$ (no $1$) |
| **Ring with unity** | multiplicative identity $1$ | $\mathbb{Z}$ |
| **Commutative ring** | $ab=ba$ | $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ |
| **Integral domain** | no zero divisors | $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ |
| **UFD** | unique factorization into irreducibles | $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ |
| **PID** | every ideal is principal | $\mathbb{Z}$, $k[x]$ |
| **Euclidean domain** | division algorithm | $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}[i]$ |
| **Field** | every nonzero element invertible | $\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{F}_p$ |

The chain is strict:
$$
\text{fields} \subsetneq \text{Euclidean domains} \subsetneq \text{PIDs} \subsetneq \text{UFDs} \subsetneq \text{integral domains} \subsetneq \text{commutative rings}.
$$
For example $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$ is an integral domain but not a UFD ($6=2\cdot3=(1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5})$).

### 2.3 Modules, Vector Spaces, and Algebras

- **Module over a ring $R$:** an abelian group $M$ with a scalar action $R\times M\to M$ satisfying the module axioms. $R=\mathbb{Z}$ recovers abelian groups; $R$ a field gives a **vector space**.
- **Vector space over a field $k$:** a module over a field; every vector space has a **basis** (uses the Axiom of Choice in general), with a well-defined dimension.
- **Algebra over a ring:** a ring that is also a module, with a compatible multiplication (e.g. matrix algebras, group algebras, Lie algebras).

### 2.4 Lattices and Boolean Algebras

| Structure | Axioms | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Partial order** | reflexive, antisym., transitive | $(\mathcal{P}(S),\subseteq)$ |
| **Lattice** | every pair has meet $\wedge$ and join $\vee$ | divisors of $n$ under gcd/lcm |
| **Distributive lattice** | $a\wedge(b\vee c)=(a\wedge b)\vee(a\wedge c)$ | Boolean algebras |
| **Boolean algebra** | distributive + complements | $\mathcal{P}(S)$ with $\cap,\cup,{}^c$ |
| **Heyting algebra** | distributive + relative pseudo-complement | open sets of a topology |

Boolean algebras model propositional logic and digital circuits; Heyting algebras model **intuitionistic** logic (dropping the law of excluded middle — connecting to the [constructivist discussion](uncountable-infinities-diagonal-argument-and-cantor-set-fractals.qmd)).

## 3. Categories, Functors, and Monads

::: {#def-category}
#### Definition 3.1 (Category)
A **category** $\mathcal{C}$ consists of objects $\operatorname{ob}\mathcal{C}$, morphisms $\hom(A,B)$ for each pair, a composition $\circ:\hom(B,C)\times\hom(A,B)\to\hom(A,C)$, and identity morphisms $\mathrm{id}_A$, satisfying:
$$
(h\circ g)\circ f=h\circ(g\circ f),\qquad \mathrm{id}_B\circ f=f=f\circ \mathrm{id}_A.
$$
:::

- A **monoid is exactly a one-object category** (elements = morphisms, $e=\mathrm{id}$).
- A **groupoid** is a category in which every morphism is invertible (a group is a one-object groupoid).
- A **functor** preserves objects, morphisms, identities, and composition.
- A **natural transformation** compares functors.
- A **monad** is an endofunctor $T$ with natural transformations $\eta:\mathrm{Id}\to T$ (**unit**) and $\mu:T^2\to T$ (**join**) satisfying the monad laws ($\mu\circ T\mu=\mu\circ\mu T$, $\mu\circ \eta T=\mathrm{id}=\mu\circ T\eta$). Monads are the categorical theory of **effects** (nondeterminism, state, exceptions, IO), central to functional programming (Haskell) and to algebraic theories (Eilenberg–Moore algebras).

This is the precise sense in which the user's chain "magmas … monads" lives on **two axes at once**: algebraic (groups, rings, fields) and categorical (monoid = one-object category; monad = monoid in the category of endofunctors).

## 4. The Spatial Ladder

::: {#def-spaces}
#### Ladders of Spaces
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\textbf{Set} \to \textbf{Topological space} \to \textbf{Metric space} \to \textbf{Normed space} \to \textbf{Inner product space} \to \textbf{Hilbert space},\\
&\textbf{Metric space} \to \textbf{Uniform space} \to \textbf{Topological space} \quad(\text{reversed}),\\
&\textbf{Measurable space} \to \textbf{Measure space} \to \textbf{Probability space},\\
&\textbf{Topological manifold} \to \textbf{Smooth manifold} \to \textbf{Riemannian manifold} \to \textbf{Lorentzian manifold}.
\end{aligned}
$$
:::

| Space | Added structure | Key theorem |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Set** | none | cardinality |
| **Topological space** | open sets | continuity, compactness, connectedness |
| **Metric space** | distance | completion, Baire, Heine–Borel |
| **Normed space** | norm $\lVert x\rVert$ | triangle inequality, metric from norm |
| **Inner product space** | $\langle x,y\rangle$ | Cauchy–Schwarz, orthogonality, projections |
| **Hilbert space** | complete inner product | Riesz representation, spectral theorem |
| **Banach space** | complete normed | Banach fixed point, open mapping |
| **Measurable space** | $\sigma$-algebra | measurability |
| **Measure space** | measure $\mu$ | Lebesgue integration, monotone/Fatou/dominated convergence |
| **Probability space** | measure with $\mu(\Omega)=1$ | LLN, CLT |
| **Manifold** | locally Euclidean topology | charts, tangent spaces, Stokes' theorem |

Every normed space is metric ($d(x,y)=\lVert x-y\rVert$); every inner product space is normed ($\lVert x\rVert=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle}$); every metric space is topological (open balls); not conversely — the cofinite topology is non-metrizable, and $\ell^p$ for $0<p<1$ is a metric space that is not normable.

## 5. Hybrid Structures (Intersections)

| Structure | Combines | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Topological group** | group + topology | $\mathbb{R}^n$, matrix groups |
| **Lie group** | group + smooth manifold | $GL_n(\mathbb{R})$, $SO(3)$, $SU(2)$ |
| **Topological vector space** | vector space + topology | spaces of functions |
| **Banach algebra** | Banach space + algebra | $C([0,1])$, operator algebras |
| **C*-algebra** | Banach $*$-algebra | $\mathcal{B}(H)$, quantum observables |
| **Measure algebra** | Boolean algebra + measure | probability event algebra |
| **Riemannian manifold** | smooth manifold + metric tensor | general relativity |
| **Scheme** | topological space + sheaf of rings | algebraic geometry |
| **Banach/Hilbert manifold** | manifold modeled on Banach/Hilbert space | PDE, infinite-dimensional geometry |

Physics lives almost entirely in the hybrid column: Lie groups are symmetries, Hilbert spaces are state spaces, C*-algebras are observables, Lorentzian manifolds are spacetime.

## 7. Why Each Axiom Matters (Counterexamples)

| Weaker structure | What fails | Witness |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Magma not semigroup | associativity | $(\mathbb{Z},-)$ |
| Semigroup not monoid | identity | $(\{1,2,3,\dots\},+)$ |
| Monoid not group | inverses | $(\mathbb{N},+,0)$ |
| Group not abelian | commutativity | $S_3$, $Q_8$ |
| Ring not commutative | $ab=ba$ | matrix ring $M_2(\mathbb{R})$ |
| Commutative ring not domain | zero divisors | $\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$ |
| Domain not UFD | unique factorization | $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-5}]$ |
| UFD not PID | principal ideals | $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ |
| PID not Euclidean | division algorithm | some $O_K$ |
| Topological not metric | distance | cofinite topology |
| Metric not normed | homogeneity of norm | $\ell^p$, $0<p<1$ |
| Normed not inner product | parallelogram law | $\ell^\infty$, $L^p$, $p\ne2$ |
| Inner product not Hilbert | completeness | $C([0,1])$ with $L^2$ |
| Groupoid not group (categorical) | one object | equivalence relation groupoid |

The **parallelogram law** $\lVert x+y\rVert^2+\lVert x-y\rVert^2=2\lVert x\rVert^2+2\lVert y\rVert^2$ characterizes inner-product norms (Jordan–von Neumann).

## 8. Computational Laboratory

The lab verifies the axioms of finite groups, monoids, rings, fields, vector spaces, lattices, Boolean algebras, a small category, and the list monad.

```{python}
import itertools
from functools import reduce

print("=== 1. FINITE GROUP: (Z_n, +) AND (Z_p*, ×) ===")
def check_group(elements, op, e):
    S = set(elements)
    closed = all(op(a, b) in S for a in S for b in S)
    assoc = all(op(op(a, b), c) == op(a, op(b, c)) for a in S for b in S for c in S)
    ident = all(op(a, e) == a and op(e, a) == a for a in S)
    invs = all(any(op(a, b) == e and op(b, a) == e for b in S) for a in S)
    return closed, assoc, ident, invs

for n in [4, 5, 6]:
    Z = list(range(n))
    print(f"  (Z_{n}, +):  closed={check_group(Z, lambda a,b:(a+b)%n, 0)}")

print("\n  (Z_p*, ×) is a group iff p prime:")
for p in [5, 6, 7]:
    Zp = [a for a in range(1, p)]
    print(f"    p={p}: {check_group(Zp, lambda a,b:(a*b)%p, 1)}  (field iff all inverses)")

print("\n=== 2. MONOID vs GROUP ===")
N = list(range(0, 6))
print("  (N, +, 0) monoid axioms:",
      "assoc", all((a+b)+c == a+(b+c) for a in N for b in N for c in N),
      "| identity", all(0+a == a for a in N),
      "| inverses for all?", all(any(a+b == 0 for b in N) for a in N), "(false => not a group)")

print("\n=== 3. RING vs FIELD: Z_n UNDER + AND × ===")
def is_field(n):
    if n < 2: return False
    S = list(range(n))
    add_closed = all((a+b) % n in S for a in S for b in S)
    mul_assoc = all((a*b)*c % n == a*(b*c) % n for a in S for b in S for c in S)
    distrib = all(a*(b+c) % n == (a*b + a*c) % n for a in S for b in S for c in S)
    inv = all(any((a*b) % n == 1 for b in S) for a in S if a != 0)
    return add_closed and mul_assoc and distrib and inv
for n in [4, 5, 6, 7, 11]:
    print(f"  Z_{n} is a field: {is_field(n)}  (prime => True)")

print("\n=== 4. VECTOR SPACE AXIOMS IN R^2 ===")
import random
random.seed(0)
def add(u, v): return (u[0]+v[0], u[1]+v[1])
def smul(c, u): return (c*u[0], c*u[1])
vecs = [(random.randint(-9, 9), random.randint(-9, 9)) for _ in range(50)]
scalars = [random.randint(-5, 5) for _ in range(20)]   # integers => exact arithmetic
assoc_add = all(add(add(u,v),w) == add(u,add(v,w)) for u in vecs for v in vecs for w in vecs)
comm_add = all(add(u,v) == add(v,u) for u in vecs for v in vecs)
zero = (0,0)
ident = all(add(u, zero) == u for u in vecs)
distrib1 = all(smul(c, add(u,v)) == add(smul(c,u), smul(c,v)) for c in scalars for u in vecs for v in vecs)
distrib2 = all(smul(c+d, u) == add(smul(c,u), smul(d,u)) for c in scalars for d in scalars for u in vecs)
print(f"  associativity={assoc_add}, commutativity={comm_add}, zero={ident}, distributivity={distrib1 and distrib2}")

print("\n=== 5. LATTICE: DIVISORS OF 12 UNDER gcd/lcm ===")
from math import gcd
def lcm(a, b): return a*b // gcd(a, b)
divs = [d for d in range(1, 13) if 12 % d == 0]
meet_join = all(gcd(a,b) in divs and lcm(a,b) in divs for a in divs for b in divs)
distrib = all(gcd(a, lcm(b,c)) == lcm(gcd(a,b), gcd(a,c)) for a in divs for b in divs for c in divs)
print(f"  divisors={divs}; closed under meet/join={meet_join}; distributive={distrib}")

print("\n=== 6. BOOLEAN ALGEBRA: POWER SET OF {1,2,3} ===")
U = {1,2,3}
Power = [frozenset(s) for r in range(4) for s in itertools.combinations(U, r)]
def comp(A): return frozenset(U - A)
distrib = all((A & (B | C)) == ((A & B) | (A & C)) for A in Power for B in Power for C in Power)
complement = all((A | comp(A)) == frozenset(U) and (A & comp(A)) == frozenset() for A in Power)
print(f"  |P(U)|={len(Power)}; distributive={distrib}; complements={complement}")

print("\n=== 7. SMALL CATEGORY: IDENTITY AND ASSOCIATIVITY ===")
# Objects A, B; morphisms idA, idB, f: A -> B.  Represent a morphism by (domain, codomain).
morph = {'idA': ('A', 'A'), 'idB': ('B', 'B'), 'f': ('A', 'B')}

def compose(g, h):
    """Return g ∘ h (apply h first): h: X->Y, g: Y->Z."""
    if h[1] != g[0]:
        return None
    if h in (morph['idA'], morph['idB']):
        return g
    if g in (morph['idA'], morph['idB']):
        return h
    return (h[0], g[1])

ids_ok = True
for name, (X, Y) in morph.items():
    idX = ('A', 'A') if X == 'A' else ('B', 'B')
    idY = ('A', 'A') if Y == 'A' else ('B', 'B')
    left = compose(idY, morph[name])    # id_Y ∘ m
    right = compose(morph[name], idX)   # m ∘ id_X
    print(f"  {name} ({X}->{Y}): id_Y∘{name}={left}, {name}∘id_X={right}")
    if left != morph[name] or right != morph[name]:
        ids_ok = False
print(f"  identity laws hold: {ids_ok}")
print(f"  composition f ∘ idA = {compose(morph['f'], morph['idA'])}")

print("\n=== 8. THE LIST MONAD: UNIT AND BIND LAWS ===")
unit = lambda x: [x]
def bind(xs, f): return [y for x in xs for y in f(x)]
f = lambda x: [x, x+1]
g = lambda x: [x*10]
m = [1, 2, 3]
left_id  = bind(unit(7), f) == f(7)
right_id = bind(m, unit) == m
assoc    = bind(bind(m, f), g) == bind(m, lambda x: bind(f(x), g))
print(f"  left identity  (unit a >>= f = f a): {left_id}")
print(f"  right identity (m >>= unit = m):     {right_id}")
print(f"  associativity  (m >>= f) >>= g:      {assoc}")
print("  => List is a monad: the categorical structure behind nondeterminism.")
```

## 9. Summary

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\textbf{Algebra: } && \text{magma}\to\text{semigroup}\to\text{monoid}\to\text{group}\to\text{ring}\to\text{field};\\
& && \text{module}\to\text{vector space};\ \text{partial order}\to\text{lattice}\to\text{Boolean algebra}.\\[3pt]
&\textbf{Categories: } && \text{monoid}=\text{one-object category};\ \text{group}=\text{one-object groupoid};\ \text{monad}=\text{monoid of endofunctors}.\\[3pt]
&\textbf{Spaces: } && \text{Set}\to\text{Top}\to\text{Metric}\to\text{Normed}\to\text{Inner}\to\text{Hilbert};\ \ \text{Measure}\to\text{Probability}.\\[3pt]
&\textbf{Hybrids: } && \text{topological/Lie groups, Banach/C*-algebras, Riemannian manifolds.}
\end{aligned}
$$

The hierarchy is the periodic table of mathematics: each structure is a setting in which certain theorems become true, and the functors between them translate results from one setting to another.
